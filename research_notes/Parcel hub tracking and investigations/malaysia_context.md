## 1. Parcel volumes, market structure and main players

### Takeaway
Malaysia's courier sector handled about 1.57 billion parcels in 2025 according to MCMC. It is running at about 5 million parcels a day in 2026, up from 52 million parcels a year in 2015. The market is highly concentrated: J&T Express and SPX Xpress together hold about 73.5% of it, and 15 licensees handle 98% of all parcels. A fictional hub sorting several hundred thousand to a million or more parcels a day is realistic at that scale.

### Cited Findings
- [NEWS, quoting MCMC] On 24 Oct 2026, MCMC Deputy Managing Director (Development) Eneng Faridah Iskandar said parcel deliveries were estimated at **5 million a day in 2026**. More than **456 million parcels** were recorded in Q1 2026, and **1.57 billion parcels** were delivered in 2025. She said industry players are using **AI and scanning systems** to improve delivery efficiency and security. National Courier Day 2026 falls on 31 October. — [Bernama, 24 Oct 2026](https://www.bernama.com/tv/news.php?id=2624968)
- [NEWS] Parcel volume passed **899 million by Q3 2024**, against 52 million in 2015. — [Bernama, Feb 2025](https://www.bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2394097)
- [NEWS] The industry grew from 52 million packages (2015) to **1.26 billion (2024)** and was forecast to exceed 1.4 billion in 2025. The actual 2025 figure of 1.57 billion beat that forecast. — [Bernama](https://www.bernama.com/en/general/news.php?id=2485927) (search summary only)
- [NEWS, ministerial] Communications Minister Fahmi Fadzil projected the courier market at **RM6.8–6.9 billion in 2025**, citing MCMC data on a sharp rise in parcel volume over the previous two to three years. — [Malay Mail, 22 Oct 2025](https://www.malaymail.com/news/money/2025/10/22/malaysias-courier-market-projected-to-reach-rm69b-this-year-says-fahmi/195534)
- [NEWS, ministerial reply] As of 15 Oct 2025, **102 courier companies** were licensed by MCMC under the Postal Services Act 2012. The **15 main licensees handle 98%** of parcels, and **J&T Express (Malaysia) Sdn Bhd and SPX Xpress (Malaysia) Sdn Bhd together hold 73.5%** of the national courier market. Other major licensees named were Ninja Logistics (Ninja Van), Pos Malaysia, GDEX, City-Link and ABX Express. Single source, and the Hansard was not checked. — [Berita Harian, Nov 2025](https://www.bharian.com.my/amp/berita/nasional/2025/11/1476045/bhplus) (page returned 403; figures from the search summary)
- [COMPANY/NEWS, unverified] A search summary says SPX's **Bukit Raja Selatan (Klang) sorting centre** can handle up to **2 million parcels a day**. This is the only hub-scale figure found. — [Malaysiakini announcement](https://malaysiakini.com/announcement/715361) (search summary only, not read)
- [INDUSTRY ESTIMATE] A LinkedIn post put domestic CEP volume at about 600 million parcels a year. This conflicts with MCMC's figures and should be treated as unofficial. — [LinkedIn, Bernard Lee](https://my.linkedin.com/in/bernlee)

### Inferences
- 1.57 billion parcels in 2025 averages out to about 4.3 million a day. That fits the 2026 figure of about 5 million a day and implies growth of roughly 15–25% a year.
- Volume is overwhelmingly e-commerce. Because two marketplace-linked or marketplace-heavy carriers (J&T, and Shopee's own SPX) hold about three-quarters of the market, a fictional hub modelled on a large e-commerce sortation centre in the Klang Valley (Shah Alam, Klang or Port Klang corridor) is realistic.
- A plausible fictional hub size is 200k–1M parcels a day, with barcode scans at inbound, sort, outbound and handover. At even a 0.01% exception rate, that is tens to hundreds of investigations a day.

### Gaps
- I found no official breakdown of the e-commerce share of parcel volume. The e-commerce link is stated qualitatively by MCMC and Bernama.
- I found no published hub counts or throughput for Pos Malaysia, Ninja Van, Lazada Logistics, DHL eCommerce, Flash Express or Best Express in this pass. Market shares for players other than J&T and SPX were not found.
- The 73.5% figure has not been checked against Hansard or an MCMC primary document.

## 2. Courier regulation (MCMC / Postal Services Act 2012) and complaint data

### Takeaway
Couriers must be licensed by MCMC under the Postal Services Act 2012 (Act 741). MCMC deals with complaints that couriers do not resolve, through its complaints portal, and can impose large penalties. I found no gazetted quality-of-service standard with lost-parcel metrics, and no recent official complaint statistics. Pricing guidance is advisory.

### Cited Findings
- [LAW] All courier companies are licensed under the **Postal Services Act 2012** and fall under **MCMC**. — [FOMCA/NCCC via Konsumer Kini](https://konsumerkini.net.my/v2/index.php/27-aduan-pengguna-nccc/1498-fomca-highlighting-grouses-on-quality-of-postal-service)
- [LAW, per MCMC statement] Licensees that breach subsidiary legislation under the Act face fines of up to **RM300,000**, up to **3 years' imprisonment**, or both. MCMC said this in Feb 2021 when it issued a "stern warning" to J&T Express after a viral video showed staff throwing parcels. — [SoyaCincau, Feb 2021](https://www.soyacincau.com/2021/02/09/mcmc-j-t-courier-companies-can-be-fined-up-to-rm300000-for-violating-postal-services-act-2012); [Malay Mail, Feb 2021](https://www.malaymail.com/amp/news/malaysia/2021/02/10/mcmc-courier-companies-can-be-fined-up-to-rm300000-for-violating-postal-ser/1948557)
- [LAW, per MCMC, 2026] The Postal Services Act provides for action against offences involving **damage to or theft of parcels** and damage to postal and courier infrastructure. — [Bernama, 24 Oct 2026](https://www.bernama.com/tv/news.php?id=2624968)
- [GUIDANCE/PROCESS] Consumers with damaged or lost items should **complain to the courier first**, then escalate to MCMC through the **Aduan SKMM portal** if unsatisfied. — [The Rakyat Post, Feb 2021](https://www.therakyatpost.com/news/malaysia/2021/02/10/improve-handling-procedures-or-face-rm300000-fine-mcmc-issues-warning-to-jt-after-viral-incident/); reaffirmed by MCMC in [Bernama, Oct 2026](https://www.bernama.com/tv/news.php?id=2624968)
- [GUIDANCE] Under the Reference Price Guidelines for courier services, the government states there is **no mandatory minimum price**. The guidelines are a self-regulatory reference tied to **Section 36** of the Act, which requires rates to be fair, non-discriminatory and cost-oriented. — [Business Today, 19 Feb 2025](https://www.businesstoday.com.my/2025/02/19/mcmc-no-mandatory-minimum-price-for-courier-services/)
- [GUIDANCE, planned] In 2018, MCMC announced plans for a **minimum service performance standard** for postal and courier licensees, with no implementation timeline. — [Lowyat.net, 2018](https://lowyat.net/2018/174957/mcmc-postal-courier-min-service-perf-std)
- [POLICY] In 2021, MCMC announced **PAKEJ**, a five-year roadmap to improve courier quality of service and last-mile coverage, and consulted on a new courier licensing framework. — [The Sun](https://thesun.my/news/malaysia-news/mcmc-announces-pakej-a-five-year-roadmap-set-to-enhance-the-quality-of-courier-services-le7978580/); [Skrine, Jul 2021](https://www.skrine.com/insights/alerts/july-2021/mcmc-commences-public-consultation-on-proposed-new)
- [NEWS/NCCC, dated] According to the NCCC, **49.35%** of complaints against postal and courier services in **2018** concerned delays. — [Konsumer Kini/FOMCA](https://www.konsumerkini.net.my/v3/berita-pengguna/keselamatan-produk/the-star,fomca-highlighting-grouses-on-quality-of-postal-service)
- [NEWS, 2025] In a single-sender case reported by MCMC in Aug 2025, the courier had logged **55 complaints** against one sender: 18 unsolicited deliveries and 37 incomplete or failed deliveries. — [Malay Mail, 22 Aug 2025](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2025/08/22/suspicious-parcel-triggers-police-report-after-mcmc-chairmans-name-used-without-authorisation/188512)

### Inferences
- The 2021 J&T mishandling video shows that video evidence of parcel handling inside hubs already drives regulatory action in Malaysia. A hub-side system that can show chain of custody, or rebut false claims, has a credible regulatory as well as commercial reason to exist.
- With no gazetted lost-parcel quality-of-service metric, a project should describe its metrics as internal KPIs, for example exception resolution time and evidence availability rate, not as compliance with an MCMC standard.

- No recent NCCC annual report figures for courier complaints were found. The latest figure located is from 2018.
- I could not confirm whether the 2018 minimum service performance standard was ever finalised or gazetted.
- I did not obtain the full text of Act 741 or any courier-licence standard conditions.

## 3. Lost-parcel compensation and marketplace dispute processes

### Takeaway
Courier liability in Malaysia is set by each company's terms, not by statute. The common cap is **RM200 or the declared value, whichever is lower**, with paid add-on cover above that. Marketplaces run their own claim and refund flows, which rely on photo and video evidence and proof of shipment. Sellers' packing videos and buyers' unboxing videos are widely used in practice but are recommended, not formally required, in the official pages found. Small consumer claims can go to the Tribunal for Consumer Claims (up to RM50,000).

### Cited Findings
- [T&C] **Ninja Van Malaysia**: liability is capped at the lower of the declared contents value or **RM200 per parcel**. Claims must be filed **within 7 days** of delivery or are waived. Paid "High Parcel Liability Cover" extends this. — [Ninja Van MY, Claims, refunds and returns](https://www.ninjavan.co/en-my/support/shipper-support/billing-and-invoicing/claims-refunds-and-returns) (search summary only)
- [T&C, unverified source] **Pos Laju**: for items posted on or after **15 Aug 2025**, compensation was cut from RM300 to **RM200 or the declared value, whichever is lower**. The optional Pos Service Warranty covers up to **RM5,000**. — [Pos Malaysia FAQ (staging site)](https://www-stg-1.pos.com.my/faq). This came from a staging subdomain, so check the live pos.com.my terms.
- [NEWS, unverified] A Selangor Journal explainer reported caps for missing parcels: up to RM100 for domestic parcels under 2 kg, RM300 for domestic parcels over 2.01 kg, and RM400 for international parcels. It is unclear which operator or instrument these come from, and they conflict with the 2025 Pos Laju RM200 figure. — [Selangor Journal](https://selangorjournal.my/?p=238147) (now redirects to mediaselangor.com; not read)
- [NEWS/legal opinion] Compensation for damage is limited to the proven value. A lawyer's example: damage valued at RM20 means only RM20 can be claimed. — [Newswav, "Understanding the rights of courier service customers"](https://newswav.com/article/A2103_6oCc47) (search summary only)
- [MARKETPLACE POLICY, Singapore help centre as proxy] Shopee recommends, but does not require, **video evidence** for faulty products. If a buyer reports non-receipt, no documents are needed from the buyer, but the request is **rejected if the seller shows valid proof of shipment** or that the item is still in transit. — [Shopee SG Help Centre](https://help.shopee.sg/sg/s/article/What-are-the-effective-supporting-documents-to-be-submitted-for-my-refund-return-request)
- [THIRD-PARTY SELLER GUIDE] Sellers are advised to **record packing videos** as evidence for Shopee claims, and to record **opening returned packages** when disputing a return. — [BigSeller, Shopee claims](https://www.bigseller.pro/blog/articleDetails/3385/shopee-seller-return-refund-claims.htm)
- [THIRD-PARTY SELLER GUIDE] Shopee Malaysia's **"15 Days Free Return"** lets eligible buyers return most items within 15 days of receipt, and sellers cannot refuse returns through statements in their listings. — [BigSeller, Shopee MY listing guidelines](https://www.bigseller.pro/blog/articleDetails/4205/shopee-malaysia-listing-guidelines.htm)
- [THIRD-PARTY SELLER GUIDE] For Lazada, damaged-item payouts to sellers depend on the seller's rate card, not on the delivering courier, so marketplace compensation runs separately from courier T&Cs. — [BigSeller, Lazada damaged item claims](https://www.bigseller.pro/blog/articleDetails/4110/lazada-damaged-item-claim.htm)
- [LAW/official] The **Tribunal for Consumer Claims (TTPM)** under KPDN hears consumer claims up to **RM50,000** that accrue within 3 years. The filing fee is **RM5**, and filing can be done online. Claims above RM50,000 go to the First Class Magistrate's Court. Exclusions include personal injury or death and some regulated services. — [KPDN TTPM FAQ](https://www.kpdn.gov.my/en/contact-us/faq/tribunal-for-consumer-claims); [TTPM Form 1](https://ttpm.kpdn.gov.my/images/webuser/MuatTurun/Form%201.pdf); cap raised from RM25,000 in 2019 per [The Malaysian Reserve via KPDN repository](https://repositori.kpdn.gov.my/handle/123456789/4330)

### Inferences
- Because courier liability is capped at about RM200 by default, most disputes over higher-value parcels shift to marketplace or seller processes. In those, the decisive evidence is proof of shipment, scan history, and photo or video. A hub system that links each barcode scan to a CCTV clip effectively gives the hub its own version of a seller's "packing video".
- Typical evidence questions a fictional investigator would need to answer are: was the parcel scanned in, was it intact at a given scan point, and who handled it between scans. Claims windows are short (for example, 7 days at Ninja Van), so evidence must be retrievable quickly, and retention must at least cover claim windows plus marketplace dispute periods. This is an inference, and the exact periods vary.

### Gaps
- I found no official MCMC rule setting a statutory compensation cap for lost parcels. Caps appear to come from operator T&Cs. J&T, SPX, GDEX and City-Link caps were not found.
- I found no Shopee Malaysia, Lazada Malaysia or TikTok Shop Malaysia official policy page stating evidence requirements. The Singapore help centre and seller guides were used as proxies.
- I could not confirm whether TTPM accepts claims directly against courier companies, as opposed to sellers.

## 4. Privacy law for CCTV in workplaces and transferring footage to a cloud AI API

### Takeaway
CCTV footage of identifiable people (hub workers, drivers, visitors) is personal data under the PDPA 2010. Since the 2024 amendments took effect on 1 Jan, 1 Apr and 1 Jun 2025, controllers and processors must appoint DPOs, breaches must be notified, processors are directly bound by the Security Principle, and fines go up to RM1 million. Biometric data is now sensitive data. Overseas transfers no longer use a whitelist but must meet one of the Section 129 routes and be documented under the April 2025 Cross-Border Transfer Guidelines. I found no Malaysia-specific CCTV guideline. Older PDP Department guidance, reported second-hand, frames CCTV as being for crime prevention and not staff monitoring, and recommends notice at the entrance to CCTV zones.

### Cited Findings
- [LAW] The **Personal Data Protection (Amendment) Act 2024** came into force in phases: **1 Jan 2025** (ss. 7, 11, 13, 14), **1 Apr 2025** (ss. 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 10, 12) and **1 Jun 2025** (ss. 6, 9). Commencement was gazetted on 24 Dec 2024. — [Baker McKenzie InsightPlus](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/data-technology/malaysia-personal-data-protection-amendment-act-2024-to-come-into-force); [ASEAN Briefing](https://www.aseanbriefing.com/news/malaysia-tightens-data-protection-from-june-2025/) (search summaries)
- [LAW] The main changes under the amendments:
  - "Data user" is renamed **"data controller"**.
  - **Biometric data** is added to sensitive personal data.
  - "Personal data breach" is now defined.
  - **Data processors are directly subject to the Security Principle (s. 9)** and face criminal penalties.
  - The maximum fine for breaching the data protection principles rises from RM300,000 to **RM1,000,000**, and the maximum prison term from 2 to **3 years**.
  - **Controllers and processors must each appoint a DPO** (s. 12) and notify the Commissioner.
  - **Breach notification** goes to the Commissioner "as soon as practicable", and to data subjects without unnecessary delay where significant harm is likely (s. 12B).
  - **Data portability** is available, subject to technical feasibility (s. 43A).
  - The **cross-border transfer whitelist is replaced** with a risk-based approach.
  
  — [Mayer Brown, 25 Jul 2025](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/from-legislative-reform-to-practical-guidance-key-amendments-to-malaysias-pdpa-and-the-launch-of-cross-border-transfer-guidelines)
- [GUIDANCE] The Commissioner adopted the **Guidelines on DPO appointment** and the **Guidelines on Personal Data Breach Notification** on 25 Feb 2025. Controllers must keep a breach register for at least 2 years. — [Rahmat Lim, Mar 2025](https://www.rahmatlim.com/perspectives/articles/30095/mykh-new-guidelines-on-appointment-of-data-protection-officer-and-data-breanotification); [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/change/13415-guidelines-on-personal-data-breach-notification); breach register per [ASEAN Briefing](https://www.aseanbriefing.com/news/malaysia-tightens-data-protection-from-june-2025/) (search summaries). Commonly reported specifics such as a 72-hour notice to the Commissioner and 7 days to data subjects were **not confirmed** in sources I read. Mayer Brown says only "as soon as practicable".
- [GUIDANCE] By May 2026 the Commissioner had issued three further guidelines after a 2025 consultation: **Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)**, **Data Protection by Design**, and **Automated Decision-Making and Profiling (ADMP)**. — [Baker McKenzie, 8 May 2026](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/05/malaysia-new-personal-data-protection-guidelines)
- [GUIDANCE] The **Guidelines on Cross Border Personal Data Transfer** were issued on **29 April 2025** under s. 48(g). One condition is enough for each transfer. The options are:
  1. Substantially similar law or adequate protection in the destination, backed by a **Transfer Impact Assessment (TIA), valid for up to 3 years**.
  2. Consent, which must be recorded.
  3. Necessity for a contract.
  4. Legal proceedings or establishing legal rights. A mere possibility of future proceedings is not enough.
  5. Reasonable precautions and due diligence, through BCRs, **contractual clauses** (such as ASEAN Model Contractual Clauses or EU SCCs), or certifications (such as APEC CBPR/PRP).

  Documentation needed includes notice to data subjects of the transfer, recipient and purpose; contracts with processors; secure transfer methods; and **records of each transfer** (recipient, country, data type, purpose, and evidence of s. 129 compliance). — [Mayer Brown, Jul 2025](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/from-legislative-reform-to-practical-guidance-key-amendments-to-malaysias-pdpa-and-the-launch-of-cross-border-transfer-guidelines); [Rahmat Lim](https://www.rahmatlim.com/publication/articles/30646/new-guidelines-on-cross-border-personal-data-transfer); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/int/legal-updates/malaysian-guidelines-on-cross-border-data-transfers)
- [LAW/standard] The **Personal Data Protection Standard 2015** (in force 23 Dec 2015) sets security, retention and data integrity standards. Breaching it can bring a fine of up to **RM250,000**, up to 2 years' imprisonment, or both. A 2015 draft proposed CCTV monitoring of data storage areas and scheduled deletion of inactive data, but **no specific CCTV retention period** was found. In Oct 2024 the Commissioner consulted on a replacement standard. — [CPO Magazine](https://www.cpomagazine.com/data-protection/malaysian-personal-data-protection-act-standards-tightened/); [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/change/9750-mcd-personal-data-protection-standard-2015); [DGC Briefings](https://dgcbriefings.substack.com/p/malaysia-public-consultation-on-standard) (search summaries)
- [COMMENTARY citing PDP Department guidance, dated 2015] According to this commentary, PDP Department guidance says the main purpose of workplace CCTV is **"crime detection and prevention"** and that it **"cannot be misused for other purposes such as staff monitoring."** The author questions this, since employees with access to valuable goods can also commit crimes. Businesses should **display a notice at the entrance of the CCTV surveillance zone**. The article also notes that the Industrial Court has admitted covert recordings as evidence in dismissal cases (2011, 2013), since relevant evidence can be admissible even if improperly obtained. — [Donovan & Ho, 4 Feb 2015](https://dnh.com.my/busybodies-in-business-is-there-privacy-in-the-workplace/)

### Inferences
- **Roles.** The fictional hub operator is the data controller. A hosted model API provider that processes clips on the hub's instructions is most likely a data processor. Since 2025 the processor has direct Security Principle duties and needs its own DPO if it falls within the DPO guideline's thresholds. That depends on the guideline criteria, which I did not verify.
- **Purpose framing matters.** Older PDP guidance frames CCTV as for crime prevention and not staff monitoring. The project should describe its purpose as **parcel chain-of-custody, loss and theft investigation, and dispute evidence**, not productivity tracking of workers. Clips should be pulled only on an exception (a lost or damaged parcel claim), not used for continuous behaviour scoring.
- **ADMP and DPIA.** If the AI model flags "suspected mishandling or theft" by identifiable workers, the 2026 ADMP and DPIA guidelines are likely to be relevant. The design should keep a human investigator in the loop and avoid automated disciplinary decisions. This is an inference, because the contents of those guidelines were not read.
- **Biometrics.** Running face recognition on footage would bring in biometric data, now sensitive personal data, and with it stricter consent conditions. A system that ties identity through scan-gun login and badge records rather than face recognition is lower-risk.
- **Overseas API.** Sending clips to a model API hosted outside Malaysia is a cross-border transfer. A defensible pattern has five parts:
  - A TIA for the destination, or contractual clauses with the provider.
  - Notice to staff and visitors that footage may be processed by overseas service providers.
  - Data minimisation: short trimmed clips only, cropped or blurred where possible, sent only for flagged exceptions.
  - A record of each transfer.
  - No retention or training use by the provider, written into the contract.
- **Signage and notice.** Put notices at hub entrances and in work areas. Include CCTV in employee privacy notices in English and Bahasa Malaysia, since the PDPA Notice and Choice Principle requires notice in both languages. That requirement comes from my background knowledge of s. 7(3) PDPA and was not verified in this pass.
- **Retention.** No CCTV-specific period was found. A project should set a documented retention policy tied to purpose, for example a rolling 30–90 days, with clips linked to open claims kept until the dispute closes. That is a design choice, not a legal figure.

### Gaps
- I found **no Malaysia-specific PDP Commissioner CCTV guideline or code of practice** (Hong Kong and Singapore have them). The only Malaysian CCTV guidance found is the second-hand 2015 reference above.
- I did not obtain the exact breach-notification timelines (the widely cited 72 hours and 7 days) from a primary or fetched source.
- I did not find DPO appointment thresholds (for example, data volume or sensitive-data triggers) in fetched sources.
- I could not confirm whether the replacement PDP Standard (consulted Oct 2024) has been issued.
- I found no Malaysian case law or Commissioner enforcement decision specifically on workplace CCTV.

## 5. Malaysian initiatives on smart logistics, tracking and AI a project could align with

### Takeaway
I found no 2026 national logistics masterplan or parcel-tracking standardisation initiative. The 2015 Logistics and Trade Facilitation Masterplan is widely seen as due for refresh. In 2026, policy momentum comes from the National AI Action Plan 2026–2030, where transport is a priority area, from MOT logistics digitalisation consultations, and from MCMC publicly noting that couriers use AI and scanning systems for efficiency and security.

### Cited Findings
- [NEWS, quoting MCMC, Oct 2026] MCMC said industry players are adopting **AI and scanning systems** to improve delivery efficiency and **security**. National Courier Day 2026 awards recognise the "fastest and safest" courier companies. — [Bernama, 24 Oct 2026](https://www.bernama.com/tv/news.php?id=2624968)
- [POLICY, reported] The **National AI Action Plan 2026–2030** is starting implementation, and **transport is one of six priority areas** for digital transformation in 2026, according to the Digital Minister. — [The Edge, Sep 2026](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/818577); [Business Today, 12 Sep 2026](https://www.businesstoday.com.my/2026/09/12/the-ai-growth-engine/) (search summaries only)

- [POLICY] The **PKFZ 2.0** masterplan for Port Klang Free Zone is a 10-year roadmap that prioritises digitalisation and **AI-driven security**. — search summary of [The Edge](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/818577) / [OpenGov Asia, May 2025](https://archive.opengovasia.com/2025/05/21/driving-malaysias-smart-and-sustainable-logistics-future/) (attribution between these two is unclear)

### Inferences
- A pitch can credibly align with three things: MCMC's stated focus on parcel security and courier quality (PAKEJ, National Courier Day "safest courier" awards), the National AI Action Plan 2026–2030 transport priority, and PDPA-compliant, privacy-by-design AI, which matches the Commissioner's 2026 DPbD, DPIA and ADMP guidelines.
- Barcode scan events plus CCTV clips form a chain-of-custody record. That fits regulators' interest in reducing theft and damage, and the PSA 2012 offences for parcel theft or damage.

### Gaps
- I found no Malaysian parcel-tracking data or label standardisation initiative, from MCMC or industry.
- I found no MDEC or MITI programme specific to AI in last-mile or parcel logistics. The official MOT document status for any new logistics masterplan was not confirmed.
- The National AI Action Plan 2026–2030 text was not read directly.
