# CCTV/Video in Parcel Hubs and Warehouses: Investigation Workflow, "Video + Scan" Products, Loss Modes and Evidence Standards (as of Oct 2026)

Source-quality legend used below: **[PRIMARY]** = regulator, official audit, carrier/marketplace or academic; **[SURVEY]** = commissioned consumer survey; **[VENDOR/MARKETING]** = product page, vendor blog or vendor case study (unverified); **[3RD-PARTY]** = seller-tool blog, aggregator or press with no traceable primary source; **[ANECDOTE]** = forum or single-person account.

## 1. How missing or damaged parcel investigations are done today (workflow, who, time, volume)

### Takeaway
I found no published, independent benchmark for hours or cost per CCTV investigation in a parcel hub. The workflow can be reconstructed from vendor problem statements and regulator material, and it is consistent across them. Scan systems (WMS, sorter) and video systems (VMS/NVR) are separate silos. An investigator starts from the last scan, works out which camera covered that point, then scrubs unindexed footage. Claim volumes are very large: USPS alone logged about 5 million missing-package complaints in FY2025. Carrier processes put the burden on the sender to start the claim, and one third-party guide notes that most rejections come from evidence format and timing rather than the underlying facts.

### Cited Findings
**The silo problem (the root of the manual workflow)**
- Hikvision's parcel-tracking pages describe the problem as WMS and video systems usually running separately, which makes it hard to pull up footage for a specific parcel when a dispute arises. [VENDOR/MARKETING] — [Hikvision HK parcel tracking](https://www.hikvision.com/hk/solutions/solutions-by-function/parcel-tracking)
- Roboflow describes the investigation it aims to replace this way: one missing carton turns into a shipper claim and a long investigation that starts with "hours of unindexed video". Its package-tracking product targets hubs where a scan at induction and a scan at load leave a gap in between. [VENDOR/MARKETING] — [Roboflow warehouse theft/loss](https://roboflow.com/ai/warehouse-theft-and-loss-prevention); [Roboflow package tracking](https://roboflow.com/ai/package-tracking)
- Marwiz Vision says plain CCTV "records video but doesn't identify which package moved or who handled it". [VENDOR/MARKETING] — [Marwiz Vision](https://marwizvision.com/package-tracking-detection.html)
- A warehouse-security firm advises indexing footage by time, door, vehicle and shipment identifier so investigators don't have to scan long stretches of video. This implies that by default they do scan long stretches. [3RD-PARTY] — [Overton Security](https://www.overtonsecurity.com/security-for-warehouses/)

**Time per investigation (only marketing figures exist)**
- One logistics video-analytics vendor (it appears to be SLIN AI) claims its software narrows a 17-hour CCTV review down to a 30-minute window, and argues that traditional CCTV review often happens days after the incident. This is a marketing claim with no method given. [VENDOR/MARKETING] — [SLIN AI logistics video analytics](https://slinai.com/industries/logistics-video-analytics)
- A trade-press piece is headlined "Parcel tracking hours reduced to seconds with integrated visualized solutions". It is vendor-sourced framing. [VENDOR/MARKETING via trade press] — [ISR Magazine](https://www.isrmag.com/parcel-tracking-hours-reduced-to-seconds-with-integrated-visualized-solutions/)
- Roboflow claims clips replace "hours of video review" but gives no time-per-investigation figure. [VENDOR/MARKETING] — [Roboflow package tracking](https://roboflow.com/ai/package-tracking)
- Footage retention is a hard constraint on the workflow. Retail sources commonly cite 30–90-day CCTV retention and review after an incident or every 24–48 hours at larger chains. These are retail figures, not warehouse benchmarks. [3RD-PARTY] — [eufy](https://www.eufy.com/blogs/security-camera/how-often-do-stores-check-their-cameras); [Reolink](https://reolink.com/blog/how-often-do-stores-check-cameras/)

**Who starts and runs the claim (carrier side)**
- DPD (UK): the recipient must contact the sender, who then contacts DPD to start an investigation. Evri: the sender makes the claim through Evri customer service, and compensation depends on the level of cover the retailer chose. Evri gives no compensation or liability cover for lost or damaged return parcels. [3RD-PARTY guide] — [ShippyPro lost-parcel guide](https://www.shippypro.com/blog/fr/guide-colis-perdu-marchands-ecommerce?hsLang=en); [Compare the Market](https://www.comparethemarket.com/home-insurance/content/parcel-stolen/)
- Reported filing windows: Royal Mail up to 30 calendar days, Evri up to 30 days, DPD 10–15 business days. These were not verified on the carriers' own sites. [3RD-PARTY] — [ShippyPro](https://www.shippypro.com/blog/fr/guide-colis-perdu-marchands-ecommerce?hsLang=en)
- One forum user offered CCTV evidence to a carrier's complaints team and got no reply. [ANECDOTE] — [TalkPhotography forum](https://www.talkphotography.co.uk/posts/9759095/)

**Volume and backlog**
- USPS OIG report 25-150-R26 ("Evaluating Mail Recovery Center Package Growth", 2025) says missing-package complaints are the most common customer complaint type, at about 5 million in FY2025. "Dead mail" (packages that can't be delivered or returned) rose 45% over a recent 12-month period, partly because shippers refused packages. The report carries 9 open recommendations. [PRIMARY; I read the summary only because the full PDF did not load] — [Oversight.gov 25-150-R26](https://www.oversight.gov/reports/audit/evaluating-mail-recovery-center-package-growth)
- The USPS Mail Recovery Center received about 67 million items in FY2019. The OIG recommended a process to check whether an indemnity claim had already been paid before returning an item. **(2020, older)** [PRIMARY] — [USPS OIG 19-040-R20](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/oig-reports/19-040-R20.pdf)
- Secondary coverage of the 2025 report says the MRC returned under 1% of missing packages, against a management claim of 39%. I could not verify this against the OIG text. [3RD-PARTY] — [RS Web Solutions](https://www.rswebsols.com/?p=134293)
- USPS OIG local audits found scan-integrity failures that undermine "last scan" as a starting point. In the Baltimore area (2021), 172 of 409 sampled packages were improperly scanned, and about 972,000 delayed mail pieces were counted, of which management reported roughly 21%. A Houston audit (Dec 2021) found package scan errors. **(2021, older)** [PRIMARY via press] — [WMAR2 News](https://www.wmar2news.com/matterformallory/audit-of-baltimore-area-post-offices-uncovers-1m-pieces-of-delayed-mail-inaccurate-reporting-improper-scanning); [Click2Houston](https://www.click2houston.com/news/local/2021/12/16/audit-claims-delayed-mail-was-due-to-management-oversight)
- UK: 37% of recipients (about 15 million people) had a problem with their latest delivery, and 47% of those then had further trouble getting it resolved (slow replies, chatbots, several contacts). Survey by Opinium for Citizens Advice, n=8,000, fieldwork 29 Aug–27 Sep 2025, published 19 Nov 2025. [SURVEY] — [Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/about-us/media-centre/press-releases/parcel-problems-reach-record-as-15-million-people-are-let-down-on-the/)
- Marketplace evidence failures: one Indian seller-tool guide says most claim rejections stem from evidence format and timing rather than the facts. Typical failures are footage without timestamps, footage not tied to the specific order ID, and evidence submitted after the claim window closes. [3RD-PARTY/VENDOR] — [Ginesys](https://www.ginesys.in/blog/marketplace-claim-proof-requirements)

### Inferences
- The de facto workflow is: (1) look up the tracking number in the WMS or carrier system and find the last scan event and its scanner or location; (2) map that scanner to a camera by hand, using site knowledge; (3) scrub footage around the timestamp, and work forward or backward across adjacent cameras when the parcel isn't visible; (4) export the clip; (5) attach it to a claim or dispute. Every vendor in section 2 positions against steps 2–3, which suggests those are the expensive steps.
- The OIG scan-error findings mean that "last scan" is itself unreliable. A system that answers "where was it last seen" should treat the last scan as a prior, not ground truth, and use video to confirm or correct it.
- Retention windows (around 30–90 days) and marketplace claim windows (48–72 h on Flipkart, per vendor guides) both favour automated, proactive indexing over reactive scrubbing.

### Gaps
- I found no published figures for investigator hours per case, cost per investigation, or claims-team headcount at carriers or 3PLs. Neither TAPA, the LPRC nor the carriers publish them in the sources I found. Primary interviews would be needed.
- I found no official carrier documentation (UPS, FedEx, DHL, J&T, Pos Malaysia) on whether or how depots review CCTV during a trace.
- I did not obtain the full USPS OIG 25-150-R26 text: the PDF URL returned 404 and oversight.gov returned 406.

## 2. Competitive landscape: products that link video to scans or transactions

### Takeaway
The market has three tiers. **(a) Packing-station "scan-triggered recording"** is a crowded, commoditised category aimed at e-commerce sellers and 3PLs. A barcode scan starts and stops the clip, and clips are searchable by order or AWB. It is single-camera and single-station, and is strongest in India and Southeast Asia, where marketplaces demand packing video. **(b) VMS/NVR "barcode overlay" integrations**, led by Hikvision, push code-reader scans (ID + timestamp) into the NVR so footage can be searched by parcel ID. This is mostly per-scan-point lookup with "no-show" alarms, not true cross-camera tracking. **(c) AI camera-based tracking across a hub** (Roboflow, Marwiz, Vimaan ParcelSCAN, iFactory, Stereolabs) claims one ID per parcel across cameras, with a timestamped image at each handoff. Cross-camera re-ID accuracy is not published anywhere, so the "follow every parcel" capability remains a marketing claim.

### Cited Findings
**(a) Packing-station scan-to-video tools (e-commerce and 3PL)**
- Common mechanism, as several vendors describe it: the packer scans the order barcode, which starts recording on that station's camera, and the next scan closes the clip and starts a new one. Order number, station and time are overlaid. Clips are retrieved by order ID, AWB, tracking number or date. Some vendors log every scanned barcode against the video so a reviewer can jump to the moment an item was scanned. Vendors describe overlays as "tamper-evident", which has not been independently verified. [VENDOR/MARKETING] — [vAudit order-ID-linked video](https://vaudit.ai/blog/resolve-shipping-claims-with-order-id-linked-videos/); [vAudit warehouse packing camera](https://vaudit.ai/warehouse-packing-camera/); [PackVOD](https://packvod.com/); [PackVision dispute resolution](https://packvision.io/dispute-resolution); [Fourio Solutions](https://fouriosolutions.com/warehouse-automation.html)
- India: ClaimVMS (dated 11 Jul 2026) describes Flipkart's "Video Management System" as mandatory for Seller Protection Fund (SPF) eligibility, with an overhead camera framing the product and shipping label and continuous recording. ClaimVMS is a vendor and cites no Flipkart policy document. [VENDOR interpretation] — [ClaimVMS Flipkart VMS guide](https://claimvms.in/flipkart-video-management-system-guide/)
- India 3PL/OMS: WareIQ (returns QC app) and Unicommerce position video proof for marketplace claims. [VENDOR/MARKETING] — [WareIQ](https://wareiq.com/resources/blogs/wareiqs-returns-qc-app-helps-sellers-achieve-claim-approval-on-returns/); [Unicommerce](https://unicommerce.com/blog/video-proof-ecommerce-sellers-claim-requirements)
- Malaysia: ParcelGuard is described as recording the entire packing process as visual proof before shipping. BigSeller tells sellers without dedicated tools to find the packing footage on ordinary CCTV by matching the photo-capture time, which is the manual timestamp-matching workflow. [VENDOR/3RD-PARTY] — [SiteGiant MY](https://sitegiant.my/?p=71857); [BigSeller Shopee claims](https://www.bigseller.pro/blog/articleDetails/1462/How-to-Resolve-Return-and-Refund-Claims-in-Shopee.htm)

**(b) VMS/NVR integrations with scan data**
- Hikvision "Visual Parcel Tracking": code readers capture barcodes and pass the parcel number and timestamp to the NVR or HikCentral, which overlays them on video so clips can be searched by parcel ID. It logs inbound and outbound times on the conveyor and can raise an alarm if a parcel fails to appear within a set window. Two deployment modes exist: cameras and code scanners on the same NVR, or integration with a third-party barcode system. Variants cover QC/packing stations and conveyors (3-Series cameras plus a customised NVR). The pages are live in the Malaysia and Singapore regions. [VENDOR/MARKETING] — [Hikvision MY](https://www.hikvision.com/my/Solutions/solutions-by-function/parcel-tracking/); [Hikvision UK](https://www.hikvision.com/uk/solutions/solutions-by-function/parcel-tracking/); [Hikvision infographic PDF](https://www.hikvision.com/content/dam/hikvision/uk/solutions/parcel-tracking/Parcel-Tracking_Solution_Infographic.pdf)
- A 2019 Hikvision brochure cites a DHL Singapore transit-centre deployment with industrial code-reading cameras and order-number video retrieval. **(2019, older; vendor-claimed reference)** — [Hikvision Logistics Parcel Tracking brochure](https://email1.hikvision.com/hubfs/EU/2019/Hikvision%20Logistics%20Parcel%20Tracking%20Solution.pdf)
- Genetec and Milestone: I found no logistics case study or product page for WMS, sorter or scan integration. Milestone has long had an event-plus-video search tab (shown for access-control events in a 2014 IPVM test), which is the generic pattern a scan connector would use. A 2026 comparison says both rely on bolted-on third-party integrations for analytics and video search. [3RD-PARTY; partly old] — [IPVM 2014 test](https://ipvm.com/reports/genetec-milestone-access-tested); [Coram 2026 comparison (competitor-authored)](https://www.coram.ai/post/genetec-vs-milestone)

**(c) AI camera-based parcel tracking across a hub**
- Roboflow Package Tracking AI reuses existing dock, induction, sorter and staging cameras. Per its own description, a detector plus a per-camera tracker is combined with re-identification across cameras using "size, shape, labels, and conveyor timing", giving one ID per package across the building. The first barcode read binds the tracking number to that internal ID, and a timestamped image is stored at each handoff (dock, induction, sorter, chute, trailer). Searching a tracking number returns the path, last known location and images. It flags wrong-chute sorts, fall-offs and stalls, and damage at handoffs, and outputs "missing parcel investigation clips" for claims and customer service. Integrations: REST/MQTT into WMS (Manhattan, Blue Yonder, SAP EWM) and sorters (Honeywell Intelligrated, Vanderlande, Dematic). Roboflow says it supplies only the tracking engine, and customer teams run investigations and retention. **No re-ID accuracy, error rates or parcel-specific customer case are published.** The named logos (Rivian, BNSF, etc.) and the "90% less time manually tracking shipping inventory" figure are not parcel-hub claims. [VENDOR/MARKETING] — [Roboflow package tracking](https://roboflow.com/ai/package-tracking); [Roboflow parcel-hub use cases](https://roboflow.com/blog/vision-ai-in-parcel-hubs)
- Vimaan ParcelSCAN captures a timestamped image of every parcel at receiving, shipping or returns, giving a searchable visual record for shortage disputes. These are point captures, not continuous tracking. [VENDOR/MARKETING] — [Vimaan](https://vimaan.ai/inventory-tracking-workflows/automated-parcel-receiving-shipping-returns/)
- iFactory case study: a "midwestern logistics hub" with AI vision at 4 zones wrote a timestamped image into its WMS at each zone. It claims shipping errors fell 65% and damage claims fell 40% in the first quarter. The customer is unnamed and no baseline is given. [VENDOR/MARKETING] — [iFactory](https://ifactoryapp.com/ai-vision-camera/logistics-hub-cuts-shipping-errors-65-ai-package-inspection)
- Marwiz Vision claims to monitor every package "from arrival to dispatch" and to identify who handled it. [VENDOR/MARKETING] — [Marwiz](https://marwizvision.com/package-tracking-detection.html)
- Stereolabs VisionLog offers AI parcel counting (counting, not identity tracking). [VENDOR/MARKETING] — [Stereolabs](https://www.stereolabs.com/visionlog/parcel-counting)
- Ultralytics reports a YOLOv5 parcel detector on a sorting line at 98.2% mAP and 82 FPS. This is a detection benchmark reported by the vendor, not tracking. [VENDOR/MARKETING] — [Ultralytics logistics](https://www.ultralytics.com/solutions/computer-vision-in-logistics)

### Inferences
- **Verified versus claimed.** Scan-triggered packing clips and the Hikvision barcode-overlay approach are simple, deterministic mechanisms and are credible as described. They match footage to a parcel only at scan points, by time. Continuous multi-camera tracking ("one ID across the building") is claimed only by AI vendors, with no published accuracy. The only independent figure is the academic ~81% (section 3).
- **White space.** Tier (a) and (b) products answer "show me the clip at scan point X". None of them, verifiably, answers "where was this parcel last seen *between* scans, and what does the evidence show?" with confidence-scored, chain-of-custody evidence assembled into a case file. That is the gap a "last seen" system would occupy. Hikvision's "no-show within window" alarm is the closest incumbent feature.
- In Southeast Asia, Hikvision's footprint and the existing seller habit of recording packing make a scan-plus-CCTV approach culturally and technically familiar. The competitive threat is Hikvision bundling, not Western VMS vendors.

### Gaps
- No primary information was found on Verkada, Axis or Genetec parcel/WMS integrations, or on Amazon's internal camera use in sort centres. Absence of results is not evidence that these don't exist.
- No independent evaluations or customer-named deployments were found for Roboflow, Marwiz or iFactory parcel tracking.
- Chinese express carriers (Cainiao, J&T, SF) reportedly use extensive video-and-waybill linkage, but I found no citable source in this pass.

## 3. Research and academic work on camera-based parcel tracking

### Takeaway
Academic work is thin and mostly pre-2024. The key industrial result (Clausen et al., 2019) tracked parcels across 37 conveyor cameras with Siamese re-ID and got **about 81% of parcels tracked correctly**, with **human interaction (manual handling) as the main failure cause**. That is exactly the situation in which parcels go missing. Researchers note a lack of open, logistics-specific multi-camera tracking datasets.

### Cited Findings
- Naumann et al. (FZI/KIT), "Literature Review: Computer Vision Applications in Transportation Logistics and Warehousing" (arXiv:2304.06009, v2 June 2023), summarises **Clausen et al. (2019)** as industry-scale parcel tracking on conveyor belts. It uses a Siamese network for re-ID, a manually labelled dataset of 3,306 images from 37 cameras containing 14,248 parcels, and a one-drive-by calibration-parcel method per belt. It outperformed classical tracking, with about 81% of parcels tracked correctly and human interaction as the main cause of failure. The review also points to Rui et al. (2020) as a promising cuboid-specific re-ID approach. — [arXiv 2304.06009](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.06009)
- The same review cites Weichert et al. (2010) on detection, localisation and identification of parcels and bins with low-cost cameras plus RFID, Hu et al. (2021) on tracking parcels inside a moving truck with multiple RGB-D cameras, and Noceti et al. (2018) on multi-camera damage and tampering detection in a postal security framework (over 90% accuracy). — [arXiv 2304.06009](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.06009)
- "Parcel Tracking by Detection in Large Camera Networks" (Springer, 2019) is cited in the TAMPAR paper. It is probably the same Clausen et al. line of work, but I did not confirm that. — [TAMPAR, arXiv 2311.03124](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.03124)
- TAMPAR (2023) covers visual tampering detection for parcels in postal supply chains. It compares parcel images at different points in the chain, which is relevant to "what does the evidence show". — [arXiv 2311.03124](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.03124)
- An early multi-camera system for real-time tracking of parcels on a conveyor belt used a stereo pair for dimensions at entry plus 4 grayscale cameras at 30 fps with overlapping fields of view. **(c. 2005, older)** — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221579097_A_Multi-camera_Vision_System_for_Real-Time_Tracking_of_Parcels_Moving_on_a_Conveyor_Belt.)
- A USPTO patent covers a vision-based conveyor package-flow management system (belt-area utilisation and parcel counts at flow entry and singulator). — [USPTO 9771222](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9771222)
- Related multi-camera tracking in logistics: Synth-Yard-MCMOT (IEEE ETFA 2024; synthetic, 8 cameras, 12,008 images, trucks), Fraunhofer IML pallet-block re-ID datasets (pallet-block-32965: 131,860 labelled images), and multi-camera worker tracking with 19 ceiling wide-angle cameras (arXiv 2510.19432, 2025; reports over 20% tracking improvement). A 2025 Lamarr Institute contribution notes a "significant lack of open, logistics-specific datasets tailored for MCMOT applications". — [Lamarr Synth-Yard-MCMOT](https://lamarr-institute.org/publication/synth-yard-mcmot-synthetically-generated-multi-camera-multi-object-tracking-dataset-in-yard-logistics/); [arXiv 2510.19432](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19432v1); [Lamarr 2025 event](https://events.lamarr-institute.org/event/155/contributions/447/)

### Inferences
- The ~81% figure from 2019 with human handling as the failure mode is the most important design input. Pure appearance-based re-ID across a hub will break precisely at manual touchpoints: jams, re-handling of no-reads, cage loading. A practical system should fuse scan events, conveyor timing and topology with vision, and report "last confident sighting" plus uncertainty rather than claiming continuous custody.
- Worker multi-camera tracking (arXiv 2510.19432) is relevant because "who handled it" is often the evidentiary question in theft cases.

### Gaps
- I found no 2024–2026 peer-reviewed paper on hub-wide parcel re-ID with reported accuracy, and no public parcel MCMOT dataset.
- I found no academic work on video as *claims evidence* (admissibility, chain of custody) specifically for parcels.

## 4. Loss and misplacement modes and their frequencies

### Takeaway
No carrier or regulator publishes an authoritative breakdown of parcel loss by cause (mis-sort vs. fall-off vs. no-read vs. mis-load vs. theft). The best-documented figures are **last-mile theft** (US: about 104M packages stolen in 2025, roughly 1 in 222 deliveries, per aggregator estimates), **delivery-experience problems** (UK: 24% of recent deliveries left in insecure places), and a trade-press **mis-sort rate of about 20 per 8,000 parcels (~25 per 10k)** against vendor targets below 0.1% (<10 per 10k). Cause-split percentages circulating online are unsourced and should not be used.

### Cited Findings
**Inside the hub: mis-sort, no-read, scanning failures**
- Mis-sort rates are "notoriously difficult to quantify but can run at around 20 per 8,000 parcels" (about 25 per 10k, or 0.25%), and are higher for non-standard items (perishables, delicates, bikes). [TRADE PRESS] — [Parcel and Postal Technology International](https://www.parcelandpostaltechnologyinternational.com/?p=4479)
- Honeywell markets sortation achieving "less than 0.1% mis-sort rate" (under 10 per 10k). This is a vendor target, not a measured average. [VENDOR/MARKETING] — [Honeywell sortation](https://automation.honeywell.com/us/en/products-backup/warehouse-automation/solutions-by-technology/sortation-systems)
- Roboflow's list of in-hub exception types (vendor framing of what to detect): wrong-chute or wrong-lane sorts, packages that fall off or stall on conveyors, and damage at handoffs. Its sorter use cases include reading labels when barcodes fail (no-reads) and verifying chute arrival. [VENDOR/MARKETING] — [Roboflow package tracking](https://roboflow.com/ai/package-tracking); [Roboflow hub use cases](https://roboflow.com/blog/vision-ai-in-parcel-hubs)
- Improper scanning is common in the USPS network: 172 of 409 sampled packages were improperly scanned in Baltimore-area units (2021). **(older)** [PRIMARY via press] — [WMAR2 News](https://www.wmar2news.com/matterformallory/audit-of-baltimore-area-post-offices-uncovers-1m-pieces-of-delayed-mail-inaccurate-reporting-improper-scanning)
- Undeliverable and unlabelled items accumulate: the USPS Mail Recovery Center received about 67M items in FY2019, and dead mail rose 45% in a recent 12-month period (2025 report). [PRIMARY] — [OIG 19-040-R20](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/oig-reports/19-040-R20.pdf); [OIG 25-150-R26](https://www.oversight.gov/reports/audit/evaluating-mail-recovery-center-package-growth)
- Mishandling caught on CCTV: in 2020 Shopee apologised after a delivery worker was caught on CCTV hurling packages (Malaysia). [PRESS] — [Malay Mail, 25 Aug 2020](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2020/08/25/shopee-apologises-after-delivery-guy-caught-on-cctv-hurling-packages-into-c/1896880)
- A logistics data vendor says roughly 1 in 10 parcels hits a delivery exception before reaching the customer (no cause breakdown). [3RD-PARTY] — [LateShipment](https://www.lateshipment.com/blog/lost-package/)

**Last mile: theft, misdelivery, insecure drop**
- US 2025: about 104M packages stolen, down about 13% year on year, with roughly $14.9B in stolen value (SafeWise). Capital One Shopping puts it at 104M, or 1 in 222 deliveries (0.45%), and about $15B. Omnisend-based figures put retailer refund and replacement cost at about $7.9B. These are survey-extrapolated aggregator estimates. [3RD-PARTY/SURVEY] — [SafeWise 2025](https://www.safewise.com/research/porch-pirate-package-theft/); [Capital One Shopping](https://capitaloneshopping.com/research/package-theft-statistics/); [North Country Now/Stacker](https://www.northcountrynow.com/premium/stacker/stories/porch-piracy-delivery-fraud-and-the-rising-cost-of-lost-packages,370870)
- UK (Opinium for Citizens Advice, n=8,000, Aug–Sep 2025): 37% had a problem with their latest delivery. The commonest problems were the driver leaving before the recipient reached the door (29%), the parcel left in an insecure place (24%), and late arrival (24%). League table: Royal Mail 3.25/5, Amazon Logistics 3, DPD 2.25, Evri 2.25, Yodel 2. "Never arrived" and "damaged" shares were not given in the press release. [SURVEY] — [Citizens Advice, 19 Nov 2025](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/about-us/media-centre/press-releases/parcel-problems-reach-record-as-15-million-people-are-let-down-on-the/)

**Headline loss rates (weak sourcing; flagged)**
- Carrier "lost-package rates" of UPS 0.2%, FedEx 0.3% and USPS 0.5% are attributed to "industry reports … like Statista". The underlying data is unverified. [3RD-PARTY, unverified] — [ShipScience](https://www.shipscience.com/?p=33594)
- "About 1.7M packages lost or stolen per day in the US (620M/yr)" circulates widely and is attributed to Security.org research. I could not trace it to a primary source. [3RD-PARTY, unverified] — [ShipAid](https://www.shipaid.com/blog/how-many-packages-are-lost-each-year-ecd9e)
- Online cause splits (e.g. "30% porch theft, 15% misrouting, 10% warehouse labelling errors") have no cited source and should **not** be used. Another site's "theft ≈17% of lost-package incidents" is self-described as a derived estimate. [UNRELIABLE] — [FreightAmigo](https://www.freightamigo.com/en/blog/logistics/protecting-your-packages-a-comprehensive-guide-to-handling-lost-or-stolen-deliveries/); [DeliveryAmigo](https://www.deliveryamigo.com/blog/how-to-handle-and-prevent-lost-packages)

### Inferences
- At about 25 mis-sorts per 10k, a hub handling 200k parcels a day would generate about 500 mis-sorts a day. Most recirculate or get corrected, but each one is a candidate "lost" parcel until it is found. This is likely the largest in-hub source of "where is it?" queries, while theft dominates the last mile. This is an inference from a single trade-press rate, not measured data.
- For the system design, in-hub loss modes (mis-sort, fall-off or jam, no-read set-aside, mis-load, left in cage) are all **location** questions that video can answer. Last-mile theft and misdelivery happen outside hub cameras, so the hub system's role there is to prove the parcel left intact on the correct trailer, which is a chain-of-custody handoff.

### Gaps
- I found no published breakdown by cause from any carrier, postal regulator, insurer or TAPA for 2024–2026. Ofcom's own lost-parcel data wasn't found in this pass.
- I found no published mis-load (wrong trailer) rates, conveyor fall-off rates, or internal-theft shares for parcel hubs.
- No Malaysian, Southeast Asian or MCMC loss-rate statistics were found.
- Cost per claim: I found no reliable figure. A "$4B in lost goods and claims in 2025" figure circulates in vendor blogs without a primary source.

## 5. Evidence standards for courier claims and marketplace disputes

### Takeaway
Marketplaces in India and Southeast Asia have made **continuous, unedited, order-ID-visible video** the de facto evidence standard. Sellers need packing video showing the AWB or barcode, buyers or sellers need unboxing or return-opening video, and claim windows are tight (48–72 h on Flipkart per vendor guides). Almost all detail comes from seller-tool vendors rather than official policy pages, and Lazada's requirement is contested. Courier claims in the UK and US remain sender-initiated and declared-value or cover-dependent, and I found no official carrier standard for CCTV evidence.

### Cited Findings
- **Shopee (Malaysia/Philippines):** claim guidance asks for proof taken before shipment, namely photos of the item before packing and of the packed item with a readable air waybill. For damage or wrong-item disputes, seller guides recommend packing video that shows function checks and clearly shows the barcode or QR. Return-opening video should be continuous, show the order code and all sides of the parcel and the defect, and show "no signs of editing". Defective electronics need a video of the product operating. ### Inferences
- The recurring acceptance criteria across marketplaces are **(1)** continuity (no cuts or edits), **(2)** the identifier visible in frame (AWB, barcode or order ID), **(3)** a trustworthy timestamp, **(4)** the full object visible (all sides), and **(5)** submission within the window. A hub "last seen" system should export clips that meet the same tests: original unedited segment plus hash, burned-in or sidecar scan ID and time, camera ID, and a contiguous span covering the handoff.
- Packing video proves the state at origin only. Hub video that proves custody and condition at each handoff extends the same evidence logic into the carrier network, where today there is no standard at all.

### Gaps
- Official Shopee, Lazada, TikTok Shop, Amazon and Flipkart policy pages were not retrieved. All requirements above are second-hand and need checking against Seller Centre and help-centre text.
- I found no carrier (UPS, FedEx, DHL, Pos Malaysia, J&T, Ninja Van) policy on accepting CCTV as claim evidence, and no court or regulator guidance on parcel CCTV admissibility.
- I found no data on what share of marketplace disputes are won or lost on the strength of video evidence.
