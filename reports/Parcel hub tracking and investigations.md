# Closing the scan gaps where parcels vanish

Inside a modern parcel hub, machines identify a parcel at only a few points: the induction scan tunnel (often with dimensioning, weighing and images), sometimes a ship-sorter scan at the outbound end, and handheld scans at unload, container close and trailer load. Between those points the sorter knows the parcel only as an encoder position until it drops into a chute. After that, the parcel's location is **inherited from whatever bag, cage or trailer it was scanned into**. Missing-parcel investigations today start from the "last scan", map that scanner to a camera from site knowledge, and scrub unindexed footage. No independent figure exists for time per case; the only number (a 17-hour review cut to 30 minutes) is vendor marketing. The standards already give Spoor a usable event grammar. GS1 EPCIS 2.0's ObjectEvent and AggregationEvent, with ADD/OBSERVE/DELETE actions and the readPoint-versus-bizLocation split, map cleanly onto scans, container nesting and CCTV observations. UPU EMSEVT's "into sorting centre / out of sorting centre" pair (EDD/EDE) formalises the "scanned in but never out" pattern. The numbers that should shape the design are these: Amazon's own sites read only **80–90% of labels on the first pass** against a 98% target; the best published multi-camera parcel re-identification tracked **about 81% of parcels correctly** and failed mostly where humans handled parcels; trade press puts mis-sorts at **about 25 per 10,000**; and CCTV recorders can drift **seconds per week**. Malaysia moved **1.57 billion parcels in 2025** and about **5 million a day in 2026**. Courier liability there is capped at about RM200 by company terms. A 2025 PDPA overhaul raised fines to RM1 million, made biometrics sensitive data, and regulates sending clips to an overseas model API. For Spoor, this means six changes: an identity-bearing `ScanEvent` contract kept separate from `SensorEvent`; time-bounded container-membership and identifier-alias records; clock domains whose offset and drift are fitted from scan-to-video anchor events; a verifier that grades "last seen" by evidence class instead of returning one answer; a staged Blender dataset built around the specific ways parcels go missing; and positioning in the gap between Hikvision-style barcode overlays and AI vendors' unverified "follow every parcel" claims.

*How to read the evidence.* Facts cited to standards bodies (GS1, UPU), regulators and auditors (MCMC, USPS OIG, KPDN), statutes and law-firm analysis, peer-reviewed or thesis work, and SAE/SWGDE forensic literature are treated as verified. Claims from product pages, vendor blogs and vendor case studies are labelled **vendor claim**. Claims from seller-tool blogs, aggregators and consumer guides are labelled **secondary**. Anything not directly sourced, including all Spoor design proposals, is labelled **inference**.

## A hub visit spans a dozen stations but only two to four item scans

Synthesised from vendor material and patents (no vendor-neutral standard for in-hub stages was found), the physical chain runs: trailer unload, singulation and orientation, induction onto a cross-belt or tilt-tray sorter, an automated scan/dimension/weigh step in a tunnel, discharge into a chute, consolidation into a bag, roll cage or pallet, staging, and outbound load ([BEUMER, vendor](https://www.beumergroup.com/knowledge/cep/7-workflows-where-robotics-can-optimise-parcel-hub-operations/); [Dematic brochure, vendor](https://www.dematic.com/content/dam/dematic/downloads/brochures/NA_BR-1070_Dematic-Sortation-Solutions.pdf)). Patent language confirms that a sorter outlet "can be a conveyor, chute, bag, roller cage, or any other container that receives all the parcels addressed to the same destination" ([USPTO 11958080](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11958080)). Manual induction stations top out at roughly **1,500–2,000 items per hour** each ([Motion Tech, vendor](https://www.motiontech.co.uk/sorter-induction)). Cognex describes the ship sorter as "often the last scanning point prior to going onto a truck. No reads at this point means re-circulation and manual handling" ([Cognex, vendor](https://www.cognex.com/industries/logistics/sortation/ship-sorter-scanning)).

At the network level, the UPU EMSEVT V3 standard models only facility-level events: posting (EMA), office-of-exchange arrival and departure (EMB/EMC, EMD/EMF), **EDD "item into sorting centre" and EDE "item out of sorting centre"**, delivery-office arrival (EMG), out for delivery (EDG), unsuccessful delivery (EMH) and final delivery (EMI). The despatch-level messages PREDES/RESDES carry a despatch → receptacle → item hierarchy ([UPU/EMS EMSEVT V3 deck, 2017](https://www.ems.post/sites/default/files/content-block-files/EMSEVT%20V3%20part%202.pdf)). UPU compliance notes say EMA and EMI normally occur once per item, while EDD, EDE and EMH may legitimately repeat ([UPU repeated-events note](https://upu.int/getmedia/405ea48f-01e3-4a6c-86f2-611142cf619c/complianceWithUpuStandardsExplanatoryNotesEMSEVTRepeatedEventsEn.pdf)). Nothing in the public standards describes stations *inside* a hub. That layer lives in proprietary warehouse-control-system (WCS) and sorter logs.

### Exception lanes are where the scan chain breaks or resets

A parcel the tunnel cannot read is recirculated, using up another sorter carrier, or diverted to a no-read lane for manual encoding or relabelling and then re-induction ([Dematic, vendor](https://www.dematic.com/content/dam/dematic/downloads/brochures/NA_BR-1070_Dematic-Sortation-Solutions.pdf)). A generic recirculation SOP has the operator review cartons coming back from the loop and decide whether to re-induct them or send them to exception handling ([MangoApps SOP template](https://www.mangoapps.com/templates/sop/sortation-induction-standard-operating-procedure)). Vision systems pull oversized, damaged, leaking or unreadable parcels from the main flow for manual handling ([BEUMER, vendor](https://www.beumergroup.com/knowledge/cep/7-workflows-where-robotics-can-optimise-parcel-hub-operations/)). Vitronic has offered "video coding" since 1995: images of unread labels go to workstations, staff key in the data, and the result is assigned to the parcel during sorting ([Vitronic, vendor](https://www.vitronic.com/en-us/vitronics-success-story-logistic-automation)). USPS's scan codes show these branches as distinct events, including **MS Missort, MC Miscode, 46 Duplicate label ID, 12 Visible damage, LX/OX Processing exception, 32 Disposed and 33 Sent to mail recovery center** ([Pitney Bowes USPS code table](https://docs.shippingapi.pitneybowes.com/reference/tracking-codes.html)). Returns run the same physical flow with their own codes: Pitney Bowes's normalised RTS (return to sender) class, plus AfterShip's exception subtags ([AfterShip sub-status, secondary](https://support.aftership.com/en/articles/15441382-learn-about-delivery-sub-status)). No carrier-published "hospital line" or overflow-chute procedure was found.

### Scan density is low and blind periods are predictable

Customer-facing feeds compress a hub visit into one "processed" event, or an arrival plus a departure. Consumer guides note that a parcel can pass through a facility with only entry and exit scans ([Parcel Monitor, secondary](https://www.parcelmonitor.com/blog/lost-package-claims-guide)). No published distribution of in-hub inter-scan intervals exists for any carrier. The automated scan points are the induction tunnel, DWS stations, sorter divert confirmation (a controller event, not a customer scan) and the ship sorter. Unload, manual encode, damage, bag or cage close, and trailer load are handheld. The predictable blind windows are therefore door-to-induction (unload piles and the singulation queue), recirculation loops, no-read and exception benches, chute-to-container-close, staging and dock dwell, and linehaul, where only trailer or despatch events exist **(inference)**. These are exactly the windows Spoor's camera evidence must cover.

## Event models converge on item, code, time, place and container

Every public scan model has the same flat core: an item identifier, an event code, an event timestamp, a location, and optional reason, action and container fields. EMSEVT's EDB event, for example, requires `item-identification`, `destination-country`, `event-date-time` and `receiving-OE`, and marks other elements as conditionally mandatory, recommended or conditional ([UPU/EMS deck](https://www.ems.post/sites/default/files/content-block-files/EMSEVT%20V3%20part%202.pdf)). UPU compliance rules also reference `item-weight`, reason codes (code list 112) kept separate from action-taken codes (code list 113), `despatch-number`, `signatory-name`, and IMPC office codes ([UPU EMSEVT compliance notes v2.0, 2025](https://www.upu.int/getmedia/e8c5a980-cd96-4b0b-8ba5-ddd3adb8a02c/complianceWithUpuStandardsExplanatoryNotesEMSEVTEn.pdf)). UPU defines a repeated event as one with the same item ID, event code, location code and origin and destination EDI addresses. That is a ready-made deduplication key ([UPU repeated-events note](https://upu.int/getmedia/405ea48f-01e3-4a6c-86f2-611142cf619c/complianceWithUpuStandardsExplanatoryNotesEMSEVTRepeatedEventsEn.pdf)).

Carrier and aggregator schemes add a normalisation layer. Pitney Bowes maps raw USPS codes to `l1Code` classes (DLD, DAT, OFD, TRD, UDL, RTS, DYU, and others) and exposes both `eventCode` and `StandardizedEventCode` ([Pitney Bowes](https://docs.shippingapi.pitneybowes.com/reference/tracking-codes.html)). AfterShip uses tag plus subtag, such as InTransit_003 "Arrival scan" and Exception_013 "Shipment lost" ([AfterShip, secondary](https://support.aftership.com/en/articles/15441382-learn-about-delivery-sub-status)). DHL's Unified API has only five status codes, and its event stream can arrive **out of order**: a "pre-transit" event for Deutsche Post parcels can land after transit or delivered events ([DHL developer support](https://support-developer.dhl.com/support/solutions/articles/47001271948-i-m-receiving-a-delayed-pre-transit-status-event-once-the-shipment-is-already-in-transit-or-deli)). USPS even encodes the investigation lifecycle in its event stream: **VM "Missing mail search request initiated", VO closed, VX "Package research case closed"** ([Pitney Bowes](https://docs.shippingapi.pitneybowes.com/reference/tracking-codes.html)). J&T, Ninja Van and Pos Malaysia expose only free-text statuses publicly, such as "Arrived at Sorting Hub" and "Processed at J&T Express" ([Ship24, secondary](https://ship24.com/couriers/jt-express-tracking); [ParcelPanel, secondary](https://www.parcelpanel.com/couriers/ninjavan-my/)). Device ID, operator ID, chute, door and image references certainly live in internal WMS and sorter logs, but no public source documents them for any carrier.

### EPCIS 2.0 supplies the grammar Spoor needs, minus a "sorting" step

GS1 EPCIS 2.0 (ratified June 2022; latest edition 2.0.1, 1 July 2025) defines five event types: ObjectEvent, AggregationEvent, TransactionEvent, TransformationEvent and AssociationEvent ([EPCIS 2.0](https://ref.gs1.org/standards/epcis/); [EPCIS archive](https://ref.gs1.org/standards/epcis/archive)). Five of its semantics map almost one-to-one onto Spoor's evidence model:

- **Two clocks.** `eventTime` is when the capturing application says the event occurred. `recordTime` is when the repository recorded it, a "bookkeeping mechanism". `eventTimeZoneOffset` is business context only.
- **Two locations.** `readPoint` is "where objects were at the time of the EPCIS event", a retrospective claim. `bizLocation` is "where an object is assumed to be following an EPCIS event until it is reported to be at a different Business Location", a prospective one.
- **Three actions.** ObjectEvent OBSERVE is "a simple observation", and the standard explicitly allows recording that "an expected observation failed to occur". AggregationEvent ADD/OBSERVE/DELETE builds, observes and breaks down containment, which carries "a strong physical relationship … such that they will all occupy the same location at the same time, until such time as they are disaggregated". An OBSERVE may omit children or the parent when they were not seen.
- **No edits.** Errors are corrected with an `errorDeclaration` (reason, `correctiveEventIDs`). Events are never deleted or modified.
- **Raw data by link.** The "how" dimension, `sensorElementList`, links raw data by URI (`rawData`), documents the algorithm used (`dataProcessingMethod`), and recommends IEEE 1588 time synchronisation.

All of these come from [EPCIS 2.0 §7.3–7.4](https://ref.gs1.org/standards/epcis/). CBV 2.0's business-step vocabulary has arriving, unloading, loading, departing, staging_outbound, holding, inspecting and repackaging, but **no `sorting` step**. Its dispositions include container_closed, container_open, in_progress, in_transit, damaged, stolen and unknown ([CBV 2.0](https://ref.gs1.org/standards/cbv/)). The EPCIS REST examples use namespaced custom business steps, so a custom `sorting` or `induct` URI is legitimate. A text search of both standards found no camera, video or image construct, and no prior art linking video evidence to EPCIS events turned up. Spoor's mapping of CCTV observations onto readPoint, OBSERVE and sensorElement would therefore be novel. This conclusion rests on a limited search.

## One parcel carries several IDs and inherits its container's location

A Malaysian hub will see carrier waybill numbers, mostly Code 128, alongside international postal items with UPU S10 identifiers and, less often, GS1 SSCCs. The S10 identifier has 13 characters: a two-letter service indicator (EA–EZ for EMS, CA–CZ for parcel post, RA–RZ for registered, and so on), an 8-digit serial, a **weighted modulus-11 check digit (weights 8 6 4 2 3 5 9 7)**, and an ISO country code. It must be printed in Code 128 (strongly recommended) or Code 39, and S10-12 names no 2D symbology ([UPU S10-12](https://upu.int/UPU/media/upu/files/postalSolutions/programmesAndServices/standards/S10-12.pdf)). An SSCC has 18 digits behind AI (00), with a GS1 mod-10 check digit, printed in GS1-128. It can identify "a case, pallet or parcel" and may be reused one year after its last use ([GS1 UK](https://www.gs1uk.org/support-training/help-to-use-and-implement-gs1-standards/logistics-labels-and-ssccs); [GS1 AU](https://www.gs1au.org/standards/id-keys/sscc)). GS1 Logistic Label Guideline 1.3 (2019) allows a supplementary GS1 DataMatrix or QR code next to the mandatory GS1-128 symbol, and leaves the AI (403) routing code's content "at the discretion of the carrier" ([GS1 Logistic Label Guideline 1.3](https://ref.gs1.org/guidelines/logistic-label/)). An SSCC can also be written as a GS1 Digital Link URI under the `/00/` path ([GS1 DL URI Syntax 1.4.0](https://ref.gs1.org/standards/digital-link/uri-syntax/1.4.0)). Sunrise 2027, however, is a retail point-of-sale 2D transition and says nothing about shipping labels ([GS1 US](https://www.gs1us.org/industries-and-insights/by-topic/sunrise-2027)). Carrier 2D symbols such as UPS MaxiCode and FedEx PDF417 are documented only by secondary sources here. The practical conclusion is that **1D Code 128 / GS1-128 remains the sort identifier**, and Spoor should store a list of identifiers per parcel, each tagged with its scheme, rather than a single tracking number. Both check-digit algorithms let Spoor reject impossible strings read by camera OCR before they reach the fusion step **(inference)**.

### First-pass read rates are far below the marketing figure

Cognex markets **"up to 99.9%"** read rates for multi-sided tunnels **(vendor claim)** ([Cognex](https://www.cognex.com/industries/logistics/sortation/ship-sorter-scanning)). The only operator figure, from an MIT thesis built on Amazon data, reports large cross-belt sites averaging **"around 80-90% read rate success" against a 98% target**, with "little visibility" into causes. The project classified no-read images with ResNet models and estimated savings of about $2.2M a year ([MIT DSpace, Dumont 2021](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/139395)). Listed causes are presentation problems: glare, folded, wrinkled or glossy labels, skew, low contrast, poor print, and belt speeds above 3 m/s ([Vitronic, vendor](https://www.vitronic.com/en-us/blog/smart-logistics/inbound-scanning-performance)). BEUMER separates "readable, but unsolvable" parcels, where the code decodes but the data is invalid or does not match a record, from true no-reads ([BEUMER](https://knowledge.beumergroup.com/cep/ecommerce-labelling-parcels)). Spoor should therefore model four read outcomes: `read`, `no_read`, `no_solve` and `multi_read`. If the Amazon figure generalises, which is uncertain because it is one operator in 2021, then 10–20% of items at some sites pass the tunnel without a machine identity. That gap is exactly where camera fusion is useful **(inference)**.

### Nesting turns a parcel's location into a recursive lookup

Containers are scanned like items. The UPU S9 receptacle ID has 29 characters: a 20-character despatch ID (origin and destination IMPC codes, mail category, subclass, year, despatch number), a receptacle serial, an indicator for the highest-numbered receptacle, a registered/insured flag, and the gross weight in hectograms. Transit operators assign receptacles "by scanning the barcoded receptacle ID" ([UPU S9](https://upu.int/UPU/media/upu/files/postalSolutions/programmesAndServices/standards/S09TechnialStandard.pdf)). USPS records C1 "Container close" and P1 "Placard assign" as scan events ([Pitney Bowes](https://docs.shippingapi.pitneybowes.com/reference/tracking-codes.html)). In EPCIS terms, putting a parcel into a bag is an AggregationEvent ADD, a cage onto a trailer is another ADD, and breakdown is a DELETE. The AggregationEvent's `bizLocation` holds "until contradicted by a subsequent event". AssociationEvent, new in 2.0, can attach a container to a *physical location* such as a dock door ([EPCIS 2.0 §7.4.3, §7.4.6](https://ref.gs1.org/standards/epcis/)). A parcel's current location is therefore the latest `bizLocation` of its innermost open container, unless a direct item event postdates it, resolved recursively up the nesting tree **(inference)**. EPCIS also lets a camera that sees a cage enter a trailer, without reading any parcel labels, emit an OBSERVE event with only `parentID`. Identifier schemes for roll cages (GRAI), ULDs and trailers were not verified.

## Sorters know position to the millisecond, but only until the chute

Camera tunnels and DWS units already produce a per-parcel record. Vitronic's VIPAC "delivers whole images, which are archived together with parcel information, weight and volume" **(vendor claim)** ([Vitronic](https://www.vitronic.com/en-us/vitronics-success-story-logistic-automation)). SICK's hybrid DWS images up to five sides at up to **3.0 m/s**, dimensions with 2D laser scanners, weighs with a WIPOTEC cell, and identifies barcodes, matrix codes or RFID at up to 4 m/s **(vendor claim)** ([SICK](https://s.sick.com/CeMAT-Australia-2024-DWS)). Cognex DataMan readers buffer and record images and can push them by FTP ([Cognex docs](https://docs.cognex.com/mx_100/web/EN/SetupTool_Manual/Content/Topics/SetupToolII/BufferingTransfer.htm)). A Zebra tunnel "captures and stores video whenever a non-read event occurs" **(vendor claim)** ([MH&L](https://mhlnews.com/new-products/product/55277733/scan-tunnel)). No vendor publishes retention periods for these images or an evidential export format.

Downstream of the tunnel, sorters track by position, not by scanning. FORTNA's cross-belt uses "a high-resolution encoder [to determine] exact cell position" ([FORTNA datasheet 2024](https://fortna.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/DS-FORTNA-Cross-Belt-Sorter-1-EN.pdf)). A sort-control patent describes a tracking loop running "every 2 milliseconds", with sort-induct photo-eyes and a shoe sensor at each divert ([US8167111](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8167111)). Integrators use confirmation photo-eyes to verify diverts ([Hytrol](https://hytrol.com/products/controls/packages/?amp)). Körber markets "comprehensive piece tracking" and dynamic chute assignment, but no WCS vendor publishes an event-log schema or its timestamp resolution ([Körber, vendor](https://www.koerber.com/en/solutions/software-and-ai/parcel-and-mail/sort-control-applications)). A confirmed divert is strong evidence that the parcel entered a chute mouth. It says nothing about what happened inside the chute, bag or cage, and that is the handover point to CCTV **(inference)**.

RFID is a niche in parcel hubs except at UPS. The company tags about **1.3 million parcels a day** at origin, has readers in every US package car and delivery facility as of 2026, and plans regional sort hubs next. It claims nearly **70% fewer misloads** **(company claim)** ([Supply Chain Dive](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/ups-store-rfid-labeling-tag-smart-package/811117/)). Elsewhere, dock-door portals read roll cages and pallets ([Avery Dennison](https://supplychain.averydennison.com/assets/media/pdfs/Avery_Dennison_Dock_Door_Portal_USA.pdf)), and cross-reads between neighbouring portals are a known problem ([US7538676](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7538676)). Spoor's existing `rfid_tag_seen` sensor kind fits container-at-door events, provided it carries a door-level uncertainty.

### Clock offset dominates fusion error

Forensic practice assumes CCTV clocks are wrong. One vendor says many DVRs "drift by many seconds per week" ([Veracity via Comms Express, vendor](https://commsexpress.net/categories/veracity-ip-camera-dvr-nvr-integration-devices/)). New Zealand's Crown Law guidelines require recording "any time errors (system displayed time versus actual current time)" at retrieval, and keeping the first retrieval as the master copy ([Crown Law NZ](https://www.crownlaw.govt.nz/assets/Uploads/Prosecution-Guidelines/digital-cctv-evidence-guidelines.doc)). SWGDE warns that container frame rates are averages, that per-frame timestamps govern, and that conversions can silently change timing ([SWGDE core concepts](https://www.swgde.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/2020-09-17-SWGDE-Core-Technical-Concepts-for-Time-Based-Analysis-of-Digital-Video-Files_v1.0.pdf); [SWGDE 19-v-003](https://www.swgde.org/documents/published-complete-listing/19-v-003-swgde-considerations-for-the-use-of-time-based-analysis-of-digital-video-for-court)). A 2025 SAE study of 4 recorders and 6 cameras found burned-in timestamps off by a **mean of 2.99 ms and a maximum of 22 ms within each timestamp second** ([SAE 2025-01-8690](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/accuracy-timestamps-digital-network-video-recorders-2025-01-8690)). Practitioners report same-brand cameras on one NTP server staying within 1–2 s of each other, with larger drift usually traced to NTP having silently failed **(anecdote)** ([IPCamTalk](https://ipcamtalk.com/threads/dahua-camera-times-how-close-from-cam-to-cam.85307/latest)). EPCIS itself recommends IEEE 1588 (PTP) for sensor time ([EPCIS 2.0 §7.3.7.1](https://ref.gs1.org/standards/epcis/)). No source documents how hub WCS or PLC clocks are synchronised in practice.

The engineering conclusion is that frame jitter of tens of milliseconds is negligible next to offsets of whole seconds between the WCS, the NVR and handheld clock domains. On a belt moving at 2.5 m/s, parcels 0.5 m apart pass a point 200 ms apart. Binding a tunnel read to the right CCTV track therefore needs alignment uncertainty below about half the gap between consecutive parcels. That accuracy is reachable only by fitting offset and drift from matched events, not from NTP metadata **(inference)**.

## Investigations still start from the last scan and scrub unindexed video

No carrier publishes a hub-level missing-parcel SOP. The workflow reconstructed from secondary guides and vendor problem statements is consistent across them. A claim, usually raised by the sender, triggers a trace once tracking has been static for about 48–72 hours past the expected date. The carrier checks the last scan's location and time, searches that facility, checks transport logs and driver GPS, and reviews CCTV only "if needed" ([Parcel Monitor, secondary](https://www.parcelmonitor.com/blog/lost-package-claims-guide); [PACKAGE TODAY, secondary](https://pkg2day.com/how-courier-services-handle-lost-parcels/)). Hikvision describes the root cause: WMS and video systems "usually run separately", so footage for a specific parcel is hard to find ([Hikvision, vendor](https://www.hikvision.com/hk/solutions/solutions-by-function/parcel-tracking)). Roboflow frames the case as starting with "hours of unindexed video" ([Roboflow, vendor](https://roboflow.com/ai/warehouse-theft-and-loss-prevention)). The only time figure, one vendor's claim to cut a **17-hour CCTV review to a 30-minute window**, gives no method ([SLIN AI, vendor](https://slinai.com/industries/logistics-video-analytics)). FedEx traces reportedly take 5–10 business days **(secondary)** ([ShipAid](https://www.shipaid.com/blogs/ecommerce-shipping/managing-a-fedex-investigation-for-a-lost-package)).

Scale and scan quality both argue for automation. Missing-package complaints were USPS's most common complaint type, at about **5 million in FY2025** ([USPS OIG 25-150-R26](https://www.oversight.gov/reports/audit/evaluating-mail-recovery-center-package-growth)). Earlier OIG audits found **172 of 409 sampled packages improperly scanned** in Baltimore-area units, which means the "last scan" itself is unreliable and should be treated as a prior to check against other evidence, not as ground truth ([WMAR2 reporting OIG, 2021](https://www.wmar2news.com/matterformallory/audit-of-baltimore-area-post-offices-uncovers-1m-pieces-of-delayed-mail-inaccurate-reporting-improper-scanning)). Claims often fail on evidence form rather than facts: an Indian seller-tool guide says most rejections come from footage without timestamps, footage not tied to the order ID, or evidence submitted late ([Ginesys, secondary](https://www.ginesys.in/blog/marketplace-claim-proof-requirements)).

### Existing products sort into three tiers, and none publishes accuracy

| Tier | Mechanism | Examples | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scan-triggered packing video | A barcode scan opens and closes a clip at one station; clips are searched by order ID or AWB | vAudit, PackVOD, PackVision; ParcelGuard in Malaysia ([vAudit](https://vaudit.ai/blog/resolve-shipping-claims-with-order-id-linked-videos/); [SiteGiant MY](https://sitegiant.my/?p=71857)) | Simple, deterministic, credible; single camera |
| VMS/NVR barcode overlay | Code-reader ID and timestamp pushed into the NVR; search by parcel ID; alarm if a parcel fails to appear within a window | Hikvision Visual Parcel Tracking, live on its Malaysia site ([Hikvision MY](https://www.hikvision.com/my/Solutions/solutions-by-function/parcel-tracking/)); a DHL Singapore reference from 2019 | Credible per scan point; no tracking between scan points |
| AI cross-camera tracking | Detector plus per-camera tracker plus re-ID using "size, shape, labels, and conveyor timing"; the first barcode read binds the ID; an image at each handoff | Roboflow, Vimaan ParcelSCAN, iFactory, Marwiz ([Roboflow](https://roboflow.com/ai/package-tracking)) | **No re-ID accuracy or named parcel-hub customer published** |

The only independent accuracy figure comes from academia. Clausen et al. (2019) tracked parcels across **37 conveyor cameras** with Siamese re-ID and got **about 81% tracked correctly**, with **human interaction as the main failure cause** ([Naumann et al. review, arXiv 2304.06009](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.06009)). A 2025 contribution notes a "significant lack of open, logistics-specific datasets" for multi-camera multi-object tracking ([Lamarr Institute](https://events.lamarr-institute.org/event/155/contributions/447/)). Manual handling is exactly where parcels go missing, so continuous appearance-only custody will fail when it matters most. That supports Spoor's approach of reporting a last *confident* sighting plus explicit unobserved intervals, rather than claiming an unbroken track **(inference)**.

### Loss modes are known qualitatively; frequencies are mostly unsourced

No carrier, regulator or insurer publishes a breakdown of loss by cause. Trade press puts mis-sorts at **"around 20 per 8,000 parcels"** (0.25%), higher for non-standard items ([Parcel and Postal Technology International](https://www.parcelandpostaltechnologyinternational.com/?p=4479)). Honeywell markets sortation with **under 0.1%** mis-sorts **(vendor claim)** ([Honeywell](https://automation.honeywell.com/us/en/products-backup/warehouse-automation/solutions-by-technology/sortation-systems)), and divert-accuracy claims of 99.99% are likewise marketing. An exhibitor article advises buyers to make suppliers quote scan accuracy separately from mechanical sort accuracy ([Parcel and Post Expo](https://parcelandpostexpo.com/exhibitor-news/cross-belt-sorters-how-to-spec-throughput-footprint-and-parcel-size)). Vendors list the in-hub exception types they detect as wrong-chute sorts, fall-offs and stalls, and damage at handoffs **(vendor claim)** ([Roboflow](https://roboflow.com/blog/vision-ai-in-parcel-hubs)). Last-mile theft is the best-quantified loss: about **104M US packages stolen in 2025**, roughly 1 in 222 deliveries **(secondary, survey-extrapolated)** ([Capital One Shopping](https://capitaloneshopping.com/research/package-theft-statistics/)). In the UK, 37% of recipients had a problem with their latest delivery ([Citizens Advice / Opinium, Nov 2025](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/about-us/media-centre/press-releases/parcel-problems-reach-record-as-15-million-people-are-let-down-on-the/)). Widely repeated per-carrier loss rates (UPS 0.2%, FedEx 0.3%, USPS 0.5%) and cause splits (for example "30% porch theft") have no traceable source and should not be used. At 0.25%, a 200,000-parcel-a-day hub would generate about 500 mis-sorts a day. That makes mis-sort, not theft, the most likely in-hub "where is it?" query **(inference from a single trade-press rate)**.

## Malaysia moves 5 million parcels a day under capped liability and a tightened PDPA

MCMC reported **1.57 billion parcels in 2025**, more than **456 million in Q1 2026**, and an estimated **5 million a day in 2026**, up from 52 million a year in 2015. It also said couriers are adopting "AI and scanning systems" for efficiency and security ([Bernama, 24 Oct 2026](https://www.bernama.com/tv/news.php?id=2624968); [Bernama, Feb 2025](https://www.bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2394097)). A ministerial reply reported in the press gave **102 licensed couriers**, with 15 handling 98% of parcels and **J&T Express and SPX Xpress together holding 73.5%**. This is a single source, unverified against Hansard ([Berita Harian, Nov 2025](https://www.bharian.com.my/amp/berita/nasional/2025/11/1476045/bhplus)). The only hub-scale figure found, about 2 million parcels a day at SPX's Klang sorting centre, comes from an unread search summary ([Malaysiakini](https://malaysiakini.com/announcement/715361)). A fictional Klang Valley e-commerce hub sorting 200,000 to 1 million parcels a day is therefore realistic **(inference)**.

Couriers are licensed under the **Postal Services Act 2012 (Act 741)**. Breaching its subsidiary legislation carries fines of up to **RM300,000**, up to 3 years' imprisonment, or both. MCMC cited these penalties in 2021 when it warned J&T over a viral video of staff throwing parcels, which shows that video of in-hub handling already drives regulatory action ([SoyaCincau](https://www.soyacincau.com/2021/02/09/mcmc-j-t-courier-companies-can-be-fined-up-to-rm300000-for-violating-postal-services-act-2012)). Consumers complain to the courier first and then escalate through MCMC's Aduan SKMM portal ([Bernama, Oct 2026](https://www.bernama.com/tv/news.php?id=2624968)). No gazetted lost-parcel quality-of-service metric or current complaint statistics were found. Liability is contractual. Ninja Van caps it at the lower of declared value or **RM200**, with a **7-day** claim window ([Ninja Van MY](https://www.ninjavan.co/en-my/support/shipper-support/billing-and-invoicing/claims-refunds-and-returns)). Pos Laju cut its cap from RM300 to RM200 for items posted from 15 Aug 2025; this figure comes from a staging page and should be checked against the live site ([Pos Malaysia FAQ](https://www-stg-1.pos.com.my/faq)). Shopee rejects a buyer's non-receipt request if the seller shows valid proof of shipment, as stated on its Singapore help centre, used here as a proxy ([Shopee SG](https://help.shopee.sg/sg/s/article/What-are-the-effective-supporting-documents-to-be-submitted-for-my-refund-return-request)). The Tribunal for Consumer Claims hears claims up to **RM50,000** for an **RM5** fee ([KPDN](https://www.kpdn.gov.my/en/contact-us/faq/tribunal-for-consumer-claims)). With courier caps this low, high-value disputes move to marketplace processes. There, scan history plus video is decisive, and seller guides already treat continuous, order-ID-visible, unedited video as the de facto standard ([BigSeller, secondary](https://www.bigseller.pro/blog/articleDetails/3385/shopee-seller-return-refund-claims.htm)).

The **PDPA (Amendment) Act 2024** took effect in phases on 1 Jan, 1 Apr and 1 Jun 2025 ([Baker McKenzie](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/data-technology/malaysia-personal-data-protection-amendment-act-2024-to-come-into-force)). It renames data users "data controllers", adds **biometric data to sensitive personal data**, binds **processors directly to the Security Principle**, raises maximum fines to **RM1,000,000** and prison terms to 3 years, requires both controllers and processors to appoint a DPO, introduces breach notification, and replaces the transfer whitelist ([Mayer Brown, Jul 2025](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/from-legislative-reform-to-practical-guidance-key-amendments-to-malaysias-pdpa-and-the-launch-of-cross-border-transfer-guidelines)). The **Cross-Border Transfer Guidelines (29 April 2025)** accept any one basis for a transfer: an adequacy finding backed by a Transfer Impact Assessment (valid up to 3 years), recorded consent, contractual necessity, legal proceedings, or due diligence through contractual clauses, binding corporate rules or certification. Each transfer must be recorded with its recipient, country, data type and purpose ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/from-legislative-reform-to-practical-guidance-key-amendments-to-malaysias-pdpa-and-the-launch-of-cross-border-transfer-guidelines)). By May 2026 the Commissioner had also issued guidelines on DPIA, Data Protection by Design, and Automated Decision-Making and Profiling ([Baker McKenzie, May 2026](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/05/malaysia-new-personal-data-protection-guidelines)). No Malaysia-specific CCTV code of practice exists. The only located guidance, reported second-hand from 2015, frames workplace CCTV as for "crime detection and prevention" and says it "cannot be misused for other purposes such as staff monitoring", with notices at the entrance to surveillance zones ([Donovan & Ho, 2015](https://dnh.com.my/busybodies-in-business-is-there-privacy-in-the-workplace/)).

## What this means for Spoor's backend

### Add scans as identity-bearing evidence, not as another sensor

The current `SensorEvent` contract says it "corroborates; never identifies who or what". A barcode read is the opposite: it is the system's strongest identity evidence and its weakest location evidence. A handheld scan proves the ID was read somewhere near the device, not where the parcel then went. Scans therefore deserve their own `ScanEvent` record, a new `SourceType` pair (`scan_real` and `scan_simulated`, keeping the PRD rule that simulated data stays labelled end to end), and a `scan_event_id` back-reference on `RawObservation`, mirroring `sensor_event_id`. The fields below synthesise EMSEVT, EPCIS, carrier APIs and WMS practice **(inference)**.

| Group | Fields (proposed) | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | `scan_event_id`, `site_id`, `source_type`, `import_batch`, `identifiers: [{scheme: s10\|sscc\|carrier_waybill\|internal\|dl_uri, value, check_digit_valid}]`, `read_outcome: read\|no_read\|no_solve\|multi_read` | Multiple IDs per parcel; check digits gate OCR; the Amazon 80–90% read rate makes no-reads first-class |
| Event | `event_kind` (normalised enum below), `raw_code`, `raw_description`, `reason_code`, `action_code`, `epcis_event_type`, `epcis_action: ADD\|OBSERVE\|DELETE`, `biz_step`, `disposition` | Two-level codes as Pitney Bowes and AfterShip do; UPU reason/action split; EPCIS export |
| Time | `device_time_utc`, `tz_offset`, `record_time_utc` (ingest), `clock_domain_id`, `time_uncertainty_ms` (from the domain's ClockSync) | EPCIS eventTime vs recordTime; DHL out-of-order arrivals; handheld batch uploads |
| Where | `device_id`, `device_kind: tunnel\|dws_static\|sorter_plc\|ship_sorter\|handheld\|rfid_portal`, `station_type`, `station_id`, `read_point_id` (maps to a `zone_id` and camera FOVs), `biz_location_id` | EPCIS readPoint (where it was) vs bizLocation (where it is assumed to be) |
| Routing | `sort_destination_assigned`, `sort_destination_actual`, `divert_confirmed: bool` | Detects mis-sort as assigned ≠ actual |
| Container | `container_id`, `container_type: bag\|cage\|pallet\|gaylord\|trailer`, `parent_container_id` | Drives `ContainerMembership` |
| Physical | `weight_g`, `dims_mm`, `image_refs: [MediaId]` | Tunnel images are appearance references for re-ID |
| Custody | `operator_ref` (pseudonymous token, never a name) | Who-handled questions without biometrics |
| Correction | `corrects_scan_event_id`, `correction_reason: did_not_occur\|incorrect_data` | EPCIS errorDeclaration; preserves append-only store |

The normalised `event_kind` enum should cover the following, each tagged with whether it opens or closes a facility visit:

- **Opens a visit:** `ARRIVED_FACILITY` (door or unload scan), `INDUCTED`, `TUNNEL_READ`, `RETURN_INDUCTED`.
- **Records in-hub progress:** `TUNNEL_NO_READ`, `SORT_ASSIGNED`, `DIVERT_CONFIRMED`, `RECIRCULATED`, `EXCEPTION_DIVERTED`, `MANUAL_ENCODED`, `RELABELED`, `DAMAGE_HOLD`, `MISSORT_DETECTED`.
- **Container events:** `CONTAINER_ADD`, `CONTAINER_CLOSED`, `CONTAINER_REMOVE`, `CONTAINER_LOADED`.
- **Closes a visit:** `DEPARTED_FACILITY`.
- **Case lifecycle:** `CASE_OPENED` and `CASE_CLOSED`, mirroring USPS VM/VO.

Deduplicate on the UPU repeated-event key (identifier, `event_kind`, `read_point_id`), plus a short time tolerance.

A `TUNNEL_NO_READ` is still evidence. It has a time, a place, dimensions, weight and often an image, but no identity. Spoor should store it as an identity-less scan whose identity may later be bound by video. If the missing parcel's track crossed the tunnel at that moment, the case explains itself: the parcel went to the no-read lane.

### Model containers and relabels as time-bounded, evidence-backed links

Two new append-only record types keep the evidence store honest. `ContainerMembership` has `child_id` (a parcel or a container), `parent_container_id`, `valid_from`, `valid_to` (null while open), `opened_by_event_id`, `closed_by_event_id`, `basis: scan|video_observed|inferred` and `status`. Containers nest: parcel → bag → cage → trailer. A dock door attaches to a container through an EPCIS-style association to a location, not through membership.

`IdentifierAlias` has `from_identifier`, `to_identifier`, `linked_by_event_id`, `basis: relabel_scan|manual_encode|human_confirmed|video_candidate` and `status`. Without aliases, a relabelled parcel appears to vanish and a new one appears from nowhere, which is precisely the false "lost parcel" Spoor must not produce.

Location resolution walks the membership tree. If the parcel's latest direct evidence is `CONTAINER_ADD` into bag B, its location is the latest evidence about B, then about B's cage, and so on. The result must be labelled `estimated` (inherited) under Spoor's existing `EvidenceLabel` enum, never `observed`. Inheritance is a presumption, as EPCIS says, holding "until contradicted". A video observation showing the parcel placed beside the bag rather than in it should mark the membership `contradicted`. Each parcel `ObjectInstance` should carry its identifier set, so that `TrackAssociation` can link CCTV tracks to a scan-identified parcel. Add a `scan_bound` association status, granted only when exactly one track crosses the read point's camera zone within the scan's time window. Its signals should include `scan_time_residual_ms`, `dims_vs_bbox_consistency`, `ocr_id_match` and `tunnel_image_similarity`. Two candidate tracks inside the window keep the binding `unresolved`.

### Fuse on a fitted timeline and grade every "last seen"

`ClockSync` is currently per camera. Generalise it to a `clock_domain_id` covering at least the WCS/PLC, the handheld fleet, the NVR and individual cameras. Add `drift_ppm`, `valid_from`/`valid_to` so the record can split at NTP failures, DST changes or manual corrections, `anchor_event_ids`, and the fit's `residual_ms_p95`. Fit offsets by matching distinctive machine events to visible ones: tunnel passages to a camera on the tunnel exit, divert confirmations to a chute camera, door RFID reads to a dock camera. Record the fitted offset as part of the case file, as Crown Law and SWGDE practice expects. The demo should show the fitted offset converging and propagate `time_uncertainty_ms` into every `FrameRef` and `ScanEvent`.

`last_confirmed` should then return a ranked set, not one row:

1. **Fixed-scanner read.** Identity is certain; location is the read point; time is within the domain's uncertainty.
2. **Handheld read.** Identity is certain; location is `zone_only` (the station's zone); time uncertainty may be large for batch-synced devices, flagged when `record_time` minus `device_time` is large.
3. **Video sighting on a track** that is `scan_bound`, `verified_same_camera` chained to a scan-bound track, or `human_confirmed`.
4. **Inherited container location.** Labelled estimated.
5. **Candidate cross-camera sighting.** Labelled hypothesized.

The answer to "where was it last seen" is the latest item in classes 1–3. Classes 4 and 5 are shown separately as leads, with `unobserved_intervals` reporting which plausible zones had no camera coverage after the last confident sighting.

Add a hub topology graph, `HubTopologyEdge(from_zone, to_zone, conveyance, min_transit_s, max_transit_s)`. It lets `transitions` reject physically impossible jumps and predict where and when the parcel should next appear, which is the same conveyor-timing cue Roboflow claims to use.

### Define "scanned in but never out" as an open facility visit with explicit closure rules

The Verifier's typed claims (`last_seen`, `presence`, `transition`, `exit`, `transfer`, `order`) need three additions: `facility_visit_open`, `containment` and `relabel`. A facility visit opens with the first visit-opening event at site S. It closes in one of three ways:

- **Direct closure:** a `DEPARTED_FACILITY` or `CONTAINER_LOADED` event for the parcel.
- **Inherited closure:** a closed `ContainerMembership` whose container departed or was loaded.
- **Downstream evidence:** any later scan at another facility.

Before a visit is declared overdue, four checks must pass:

1. Resolve aliases, so a relabel is not mistaken for a disappearance.
2. Wait out a late-arrival grace period on `record_time`, so a batch upload that has not landed yet is not mistaken for a missing scan.
3. Apply UPU-style deduplication, so repeated events are not miscounted.
4. Check the downstream breakdown. If the bag's DELETE at the next site omitted the parcel, the inherited closure is downgraded to `contradicted`.

The resulting states are `open_within_sla`, `open_overdue` (an investigation candidate), `closed_direct`, `closed_inherited` and `closed_video_only`. The last is permitted only as an estimated claim, never as an observed exit, unless identity was scan-bound at the exit camera. The same machinery flags "containerised but the container arrived without the item" and mis-sorts, where `sort_destination_actual` differs from `sort_destination_assigned`.

### Stage the Blender dataset around the ways parcels actually go missing

Blender gives Spoor exact ground truth, so each scenario should emit a simulated scan log (`scan_simulated`) generated from the animation's true positions and then deliberately degraded. Two parts of the pipeline do the degrading. Clock domains get known offsets and drift, such as an NVR running +7 s and drifting, and a handheld fleet running −2 s with a 40-minute batch upload. Labels get a configurable no-read rate in the Amazon-observed 10–20% band.

| Scenario | What happens physically | What it tests |
|---|---|---|
| Control path | Unload → induct → tunnel read → divert → chute → bag ADD → cage → trailer load | Baseline closure, clock fitting from tunnel anchors |
| No-read rework | Tunnel no-read → recirculation → no-read lane → manual encode → re-induct | Identity-less scan bound by video; gap explanation |
| Relabel | Damaged label replaced at exception bench; new waybill | `IdentifierAlias`; false-vanish prevention |
| Mis-sort | Divert to wrong chute, bagged into wrong bag, departs on time | "Scanned out" yet lost; assigned ≠ actual |
| Fall-off/jam | Parcel drops behind a merge between tunnel and chute; no further scans | Last confident sighting plus uncovered zone |
| Left in chute | Diverted but never bagged; chute cleared hours later | `facility_visit_open` overdue; chute camera evidence |
| Phantom containment | Parcel scanned into bag but placed beside it; bag departs | Inheritance contradicted by video |
| Mis-load | Cage loaded onto wrong trailer at adjacent door; door RFID cross-read | Door-level uncertainty; container OBSERVE with parent only |
| Look-alike pair | Two identical brown cartons adjacent on belt | `unresolved` association; refusal to over-claim |
| Camera outage | One staging camera drops for 20 min | `unobserved_intervals` split by coverage |
| Removal into blind spot | A person carries the parcel out of coverage at staging | Object-only claim ("left coverage near zone X"); no identification of the person |
| Return-to-sender | Parcel re-enters hub as a return with a new label | Reverse flow; alias plus `RETURN_INDUCTED` |

### Keep the privacy design narrow, exception-triggered and person-agnostic

Synthetic Blender footage contains no personal data, which keeps the hackathon demo clear of PDPA exposure. A real deployment needs these guardrails **(inference, grounded in the PDPA sources above)**:

- **Purpose.** Declare the purpose as parcel chain-of-custody and claims evidence, not staff monitoring, consistent with the only Malaysian CCTV guidance found.
- **No faces.** Keep Spoor's existing rule that tracks and object instances are never people. Avoid face recognition entirely, because biometric data is now sensitive.
- **Pseudonymous operators.** Tie "who scanned" to pseudonymous `operator_ref` tokens from scan-gun logins, resolved only by an authorised human.
- **Exception-triggered extraction.** Extract clips only for an open case. Trim and crop them before any call to a hosted model such as Muse Spark. If the model is hosted outside Malaysia, treat each call as a cross-border transfer with a documented basis (a TIA or contractual clauses) and a per-transfer record. The provider contract should bar retention and training on the clips.
- **Human decisions.** Keep a human investigator as the decision-maker for anything touching an identifiable worker, consistent with the ADMP and DPIA guidelines.
- **Signage and retention.** Post bilingual signage. Set a documented retention policy, for example a rolling 30–90 days with case-linked clips held until disputes close. This is a design choice; no statutory CCTV retention period exists.

### Position Spoor in the gap between overlays and unverified tracking

Hikvision already sells "search footage by parcel ID" and a "no-show within window" alarm in Malaysia. Spoor cannot win on that. AI trackers claim one ID across the building but publish no accuracy, and the only independent benchmark (81%) fails at manual handling. Spoor's defensible position is to be the investigation layer that sits on top of whatever scan and video systems a hub already runs. Its distinguishing features are a deterministic verifier that grades each claim; explicit unobserved intervals and contradictions; container- and alias-aware "scanned in but never out" logic; documented clock offsets; and an export that meets the tests marketplaces and forensic guides already apply: an original segment with a hash, the identifier and time on a sidecar, the camera ID, a continuous span across the handoff, and the fitted offset. The steelman against this position is that Hikvision could add a verifier, or Roboflow could add case files. Spoor's moat is therefore evidence discipline and vendor neutrality, not perception. An optional EPCIS 2.0 JSON-LD export, with CCTV observations as `sensorElement` entries (`rawData` = clip URI, `dataProcessingMethod` = model card), would make that neutrality concrete.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "find where a parcel was last seen" should mean in Spoor. A hub already locates parcels well at its automated scan points. Parcels go missing in the handled, unscanned intervals: no-read benches, chutes, cages, staging lanes. Those are also where cross-camera re-identification is weakest and where scan logs inherit location from containers rather than observing it. The valuable output is therefore not a continuous track. It is an evidence ledger that says which claims are scan-certain, which are video-confirmed, which are only inherited or hypothesised, and which zones nobody could see. That structure fits Spoor's existing append-only store, typed verifier and coverage-split unobserved intervals, and it extends naturally with `ScanEvent`, `ContainerMembership`, `IdentifierAlias` and multi-domain `ClockSync`.

Two findings matter most for the build. First, clock alignment fitted from scan-to-video anchor events is the hidden prerequisite: without sub-second alignment, a tunnel read cannot be bound to the right parcel on a belt, and every downstream claim inherits that ambiguity. Second, in Malaysia the commercial pull comes less from courier liability, which is capped at about RM200, than from marketplace disputes that already demand order-linked, continuous, timestamped video. A hub-side system that produces such evidence for every handoff, while staying person-agnostic and transfer-documented under the 2025 PDPA regime, occupies ground that neither barcode-overlay NVRs nor AI tracking vendors have verifiably claimed.
