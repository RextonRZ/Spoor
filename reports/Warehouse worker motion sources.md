# Bake the parcel to the hand, not the hand to the parcel

The fastest route to believable parcel handling in the Spoor hub is not a new mocap library. It is a small procedural layer, built in about 6 hours, that **writes each parcel's world matrix from the hand socket every frame (a "matrix bake"), drives two-hand IK to sockets on the parcel or cage handle, pins the feet, and then bakes everything to plain keys**. It sits on top of base clips the project mostly already has on disk. Usable source motion is thin. Mixamo has the only convenient box clips (Picking Up, Putting Down, Lifting Object, Box Idle/Turn, Holding Walk, Push Start/Stop). Quaternius UAL (CC0) adds only `PickUp_Table` and `Push_Loop`. AndyData (CC BY 4.0) is the one rule-compliant source of real load-handling mocap. CMU has the best box trials but a custom licence that needs owner approval. No allowed source anywhere contains roll-cage pushing, barcode scanning or sack tying, so those three must be procedural. Two owner decisions were open; both are now settled (see the decisions note in the plan section): every worker uses Rocketbox (MIT) with the procedural contact layer, Mixamo is out of the render path, and CMU is not needed. At CCTV distance the viewer sees sliding feet, gaps between hands and box, and pops at grab or release; finger shape is invisible. That fixes the order of work: foot lock and rigid attachment first, timing second, grip detail last. The station plan below fits about 26 hours of implementation plus about 4 hours of realism audit.

## Only three gaps have no source, and every allowed library leaves them open

Mixamo is the practical backbone, and its catalogue is more specific than its reputation. An index of 2,446 Mixamo clips gives exact names, frame counts and motion IDs ([HF mirror index](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Linzhan/Mixamo-Animations-Characters)). The parcel-relevant set is: **Picking Up** ("Picking Up An Object", 288 f), **Putting Down** (311 f), **Lifting Object** ("Lifting A Heavy Object With Both Hands", 346 f), **Lifting** ("Pick Up Object And Examine", 217 f), **Box Idle** (190 f), **Box Turn** left and right, **Box Walk Arc**, **Holding Idle/Walk/Turn Left/Turn Right**, **Push / Push Start / Push Stop / Pushing**, and **Wheelbarrow Idle/Walk**. Take the clips from mixamo.com itself; the mirror's research-use note does not carry over. The catalogue has **no push-cart or trolley clip, no straight-line "walk carrying a box" except Holding Walk, and no scanner clip**. Texting While Standing is the nearest device pose. The project already has `Picking Up Object`, `Lifting Object`, `Carrying`, `Carrying Turn`, `Push`, `Pushing`, `Texting While Standing`, `Walking` and `Breathing Idle` in `assets/hub/build/third_party/mixamo/`.

Mixamo's licence is the single biggest risk in the plan. Adobe's help page grants royalty-free use "for personal, commercial, and non-profit projects" ([Adobe Mixamo FAQ](https://helpx.adobe.com/creative-cloud/faq/mixamo-faq.html)). A widely cited 2022 Adobe community FAQ adds: **"The only research application Mixamo content can't be used in is training machine-learning models"** ([Adobe Community FAQ](https://community.adobe.com/t5/mixamo-discussions/mixamo-faq-licensing-royalties-ownership-eula-and-tos/td-p/13234775)). That line is absent from the current help page, but nobody has withdrawn it, and users were still asking about it in 2026 without an Adobe staff answer. Spoor does not train models on its renders: the footage is test and evaluation data for Meta's models used as-is, so this clause does not strictly bite. The owner nevertheless chose Rocketbox (MIT) for every worker to remove all licence doubt and keep one skeleton family; Mixamo is out of the render path (decision of 2026-10-30).

The clean-licence alternatives are narrow. **Quaternius UAL is verified CC0** and marked "No generative AI was used" ([itch.io UAL](https://quaternius.itch.io/universal-animation-library)). Its free Standard set holds only `PickUp_Table`, `Push_Loop`, `Interact`, `Fixing_Kneeling` and generic idles and walks ([ozz-animation copy of ual.fbx](https://github.com/guillaumeblanc/ozz-animation/tree/master/media/fbx/quaternius)). The clip lists of the paid Pro tier and of UAL2 were not verified. **Microsoft Rocketbox is MIT** and ships 417 animations ([Microsoft-Rocketbox](https://github.com/microsoft/Microsoft-Rocketbox)). The project already uses its `work_mid`, `work_table`, `trolley_idle`, `hold_bag_idle`, `crouch_in/idle/out` and `documents_check` clips with no retargeting, because they share the crowd avatars' Bip01 skeleton. **AndyData-lab-onePerson (CC BY 4.0)** is the only allowed real industrial mocap: 13 participants "manipulating loads", Xsens BVH, and per-frame labels for reaching, carrying and picking ([Zenodo 3254403](https://zenodo.org/records/3254403)). The **MINES ParisTech industrial benchmark (CC BY 4.0)** adds BVH of packing TVs onto pallets ([Zenodo 5356992](https://zenodo.org/records/5356992)). Both need a cross-skeleton retarget and segment cutting, which is real work under a 30-hour cap.

CMU holds the best box trials but needs approval. Useful trials are 69_68–75 (walk, pick up, carry, set down in one take), 115_01–10 (stoop and squat box lifts), 143_11 "Walk And Pick up Box", 79_25 "moving heavy box", 81_06 "push heavy box" and 62_19 "opening a box" ([CMU search "box"](http://mocap.cs.cmu.edu/search.php?subjectnumber=%25&motion=box); [CMU search "pick"](http://mocap.cs.cmu.edu/search.php?subjectnumber=%25&motion=pick)). The licence says "free for all uses", allows inclusion in commercial products, and forbids only resale of the data ([mocap.cs.cmu.edu](http://mocap.cs.cmu.edu/)). The FAQ says the data "may be copied, modified, or redistributed without permission" ([CMU FAQs](http://mocap.cs.cmu.edu/faqs.php)). It is permissive and silent on ML, but it is not CC0, CC BY or MIT, so the rule requires owner approval. CMU also warns that hand and toe joints are noisy and that fingers were never captured.

Everything else is out. Bandai Namco is CC BY-NC ([README](https://github.com/BandaiNamcoResearchInc/Bandai-Namco-Research-Motiondataset/blob/master/README.md)). LaFAN1 is CC BY-NC-ND ([Ubisoft](https://github.com/ubisoft/ubisoft-laforge-animation-dataset)). SFU is research-only ([SFU](https://mocap.cs.sfu.ca/)). AMASS explicitly prohibits training networks for commercial use ([AMASS licence](https://amass.is.tue.mpg.de/license.html)). The most on-topic dataset found, **LARa warehouse picking and packing, is CC BY-NC 4.0** ([Zenodo 8189341](https://zenodo.org/records/8189341)). Rokoko, Truebones and ActorCore free packs use custom EULAs that were not verified in 2026, so each needs approval and none offers a verified box clip.

## A matrix bake beats Child Of for every grab, hand-over and conveyor ride

Blender 5.x ships no armature-to-armature retargeter. The built-in Global Transform tools work per bone and are manual ([Manual: Global Transform](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/editors/3dview/animation/copy_global_transform.html)). Every contact technique therefore has to come from constraints, scripted keys and baking.

**Attachment.** The most reliable headless pattern skips live parenting altogether. At the grab frame, compute `offset = M_hand(A)⁻¹ @ M_parcel(A)`. Then key `parcel.matrix_world = M_hand(f) @ offset` on every frame up to the release, with linear interpolation and quaternion sign continuity. After release, hold the last value. The hand matrix must be `arm.matrix_world @ pose_bone.matrix` taken from the evaluated depsgraph. Using the pose-bone matrix alone is the armature-target bug that Blender itself once had to fix. Keying every frame removes all interpolation pops. A hand-over to a second worker or a conveyor is just a new segment whose offset is recomputed from the parcel's current matrix. A belt is a "parent" whose matrix translates along the belt axis. The Child Of alternative works, but it pops in three ways. First, its inverse matrix is computed lazily at depsgraph evaluation (`set_inverse_pending`), and the operator is unreliable from scripts ([API: ChildOfConstraint](https://docs.blender.org/api/current/bpy.types.ChildOfConstraint.html); [BlenderArtists thread](https://blenderartists.org/t/set-inverse-child-of-constraints-via-python/1133914)). Second, a Bezier-interpolated influence blends the parcel toward the hand over several frames. Third, an un-keyed basis snaps the parcel back to its pre-grab spot at release. If Child Of is used, set `inverse_matrix` explicitly, key influence with CONSTANT interpolation, and bake with `anim_utils.bake_action(..., do_visual_keying=True, do_constraint_clear=True)` ([API: anim_utils](https://docs.blender.org/api/current/bpy_extras.anim_utils.html)). In 5.x, f-curves are reached through action slots and channelbags (`action_ensure_channelbag_for_slot`), not `Action.fcurves` ([API 5.0: Action](https://docs.blender.org/api/5.0/bpy.types.Action.html)). The project's `_channelbag_fcurves` helper already handles this.

**Two-hand contact.** Place two socket empties on each parcel, cage handle or bag neck, 2–3 cm outside the surface. Put an IK constraint on each forearm (`chain_count=2`, `use_stretch=False`) aimed at its socket, with a pole empty parented to the pelvis or spine about 0.3–0.5 m behind each elbow. Never parent the pole to the box. Blender cannot compute pole angles automatically ([bf-animsys 2014](https://archive.blender.org/lists/bf-animsys/2014-January/000130.html)), so compute `pole_angle` per arm with the Jerryno StackExchange formula, now packaged as the Bone Math extension ([Bone Math](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/bone-math/)). Keep each target inside about 95 % of arm reach so the elbow plane never degenerates. Ramp IK influence over 4–8 frames during the reach. Never let the box follow the hand while that hand's IK also targets the box: that is a dependency cycle, and it shows up as one-frame jitter. The box gets its motion from keys, from a torso carry empty or from the matrix bake, and the hands follow the box ([API: KinematicConstraint](https://docs.blender.org/api/current/bpy.types.KinematicConstraint.html)). The `KinematicConstraint` fields needed are documented. Rocketbox's exact arm chain is not: whether forearm twist bones sit between `Bip01 L Forearm` and `Bip01 L Hand` must be checked in the file before choosing `chain_count`.

**Retargeting.** The decisive point for the deadline: **put Mixamo clips only on Mixamo-rigged workers and Rocketbox clips only on Rocketbox workers, and get cross-rig realism from IK rather than retargeting**. If Mixamo or CMU motion has to reach a Bip01 skeleton, retarget in world space against a shared reference pose: `R_tgt(t) = (R_src(t) @ R_src_ref⁻¹) @ R_tgt_ref`. Write `pose_bone.matrix` parents first. Move only the root, scaled by the hip-height ratio. Biped bones run along local X while Mixamo bones run along local Y, Rocketbox's bind pose is not a clean T-pose, and Mixamo FBX arrives at 0.01 scale ([Microsoft-Rocketbox](https://github.com/microsoft/Microsoft-Rocketbox); [blender-addons #104753](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender-addons/issues/104753)). For those reasons a local-quaternion copy leaves a constant offset on every bone. The project's existing `people._retarget` (`rest_avatar⁻¹ · rest_clip · q_clip`) is sound for Rocketbox-to-Rocketbox, where both skeletons share the same Biped axis convention. Do not reuse it for Mixamo, CMU or AndyData sources. The free KBS-DEV "Retarget" extension (GPL-3.0, Blender 5+, Mixamo preset, root-motion transfer) is the best add-on candidate, but it has no documented Python API and one reviewer reported breakage on 5.1 ([Blender Extensions: Retarget](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/retarget/)). A custom script of about 100 lines is more controllable for headless use.

**Feet and loops.** Detect stance frames where the foot is within about 2 cm of its minimum height and moving under about 0.15 m/s. Pin a leg-IK target at the mean stance position, ramp influence over 2–3 frames at contact and heel-off, then bake. These thresholds are engineering estimates and should be tuned on renders. For movers, check first that the stance foot's world speed is near zero: retiming the mover to the clip's stride is cheaper than IK repair. The project already does this with `root_fwd` and the seamless Push window at frames 62–162. NLA strips expose `blend_in/out`, `blend_type`, `extrapolation` and `action_slot` ([API: NlaStrip](https://docs.blender.org/api/current/bpy.types.NlaStrip.html)). The project's `crossfade_loop` already turns every loop seam into a crossfade. Upper-body spine lean and yaw toward the target go on an `ADD`/`COMBINE` track above the base clip.

## NVIDIA's pipeline ships no pick-up, so contact would be Spoor's differentiator

The leading commercial pipeline does not solve hand–object interaction. Isaac Sim's people simulation offers GoTo, Idle, LookAround, Sit, Queue and Dequeue. Its "custom commands" play a retargeted clip in place (`Timing`), walk to an object and then play a clip (`TimingToObject`), or blend an upper-body clip over walking through a joint filter (`GoToBlend`). The only shipped custom examples are `push_button` and `type_keyboard`, and the documentation describes no pick-up or carry ([Isaac Sim 5.1 Actor Control](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/5.1.0/action_and_event_data_generation/ext_replicator-agent/actor_control.html)). NVIDIA's PhysicalAI-SmartSpaces warehouse data (CC BY 4.0, around 280 h from about 1,800 cameras) labels people, forklifts and robots, and closes the realism gap with Cosmos Transfer restyling rather than richer motion ([HF dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/PhysicalAI-SmartSpaces)). PeopleSansPeople drove 28 scanned humans with 39 retargeted Mixamo clips and no object interaction ([arXiv 2112.09290](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.09290)). The `GoToBlend` idea, an upper-body carry layered on locomotion, is the industry baseline. Spoor's Blender equivalent is an NLA upper-body layer plus the matrix bake.

The research on human–object interaction says the contact details are worth the effort. HOI-Synth found that synthetic data plus 10 % of the real labels added **+5.7 to +11.7 AP**, and that effectiveness "consistently improves with better synthetic-real alignment in objects, grasps, and environments" ([arXiv 2603.29733](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29733)). Synthetic pretraining lifted industrial active-object mAP from about 22 % to about 36 % ([arXiv 2306.12152](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.12152)). Synthetic-only models still trailed real-trained ones by 30–40 AP in a 2023 study ([arXiv 2312.02672](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.02672v3)). Spoor's synthetic frames should therefore be judged as pretraining material, where correct grasp placement matters even when fingers cannot be resolved. I found no published synthetic warehouse CCTV dataset in which workers physically pick, carry and place parcels, so there is no reference pipeline to copy.

## At 100–200 px tall, feet and the hand–box gap matter and fingers do not

Action recognition works on figures 20–40 px tall ([Efros ICCV 2003](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Research/Projects/CS/vision/human/efros-iccv03.pdf); [SDHA 2010](https://cvrc.ece.utexas.edu/SDHA2010/Aerial_View_Activity.html)). At Spoor's 100–200 px, a 1.7 m worker comes to about 0.6–1.2 px per cm. A 30 cm carton is 18–35 px across, a hand 11–22 px, and **a finger 1–2 px**, so grip shape is invisible while the box's height, its position between the palms and its rigidity are not. Surveillance research detects carried objects as protrusions from the silhouette that move with the torso ([Damen & Hogg ECCV 2008](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/74697/4/eccv08.pdf)). Any lag or drift between parcel and body therefore corrupts exactly the cue a detector learns.

Viewers notice **foot sliding below 21 mm in most conditions**, and they prefer cleaned animation regardless of how large the correction is ([Pražák, Hoyet, O'Sullivan SCA 2011](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/SCA.SCA11.287-294)). Camera motion helps hide slipping, and a fixed CCTV camera gives no such masking ([OSU thesis](https://ir.library.oregonstate.edu/concern/honors_college_theses/6395w911w?locale=en)). In third-person views of object handling, **hands floating off the surface hurt plausibility more than hands slightly inside it**. Resizing the object to the distance between the hands raised ratings significantly ([Hough et al., IEEE Access 2021](https://doaj.org/article/9460b87d47244aecabf4f8d95e8bd037)). Slightly inaccurate finger motion leaves comprehension unchanged ([Jörg et al.](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10394435)). Perceived crowd smoothness depends on update rate, velocity and motion complexity, not on clothing or character type ([McDonnell et al. SCA 2007](https://unpaywall.org/10.2312%2FSCA%2FSCA07%2F259-270)). From this, the priority order is: planted feet first; the parcel at the right height and touching both palms, with sockets biased about 1 cm inward and per-parcel width chosen to match the clip's hand span; no pops at grab, release or loop seams; plausible cadence; and fingers last. The existing `grip_device` finger solver is already more than CCTV needs. Measured thresholds for hand–box gaps under H.265 at surveillance resolution do not exist, so the close-crop audit remains the arbiter.

## Conclusion

The research changes where the effort should go. Better clips are not what is missing: no allowed library contains the three hub-specific actions, and the best datasets are non-commercial anyway. Contact is what is missing, and contact is a geometry problem that a 6-hour toolkit solves once for every station. Because NVIDIA's own pipeline stops at walking, idling and button presses, parcels that ride rigidly between two palms, land flush on the belt and never slide feet would put Spoor ahead of the leading public synthetic warehouse data, not just level with it.

The licence question is closed: Spoor's renders are evaluation data, not training data, and the owner chose Rocketbox (MIT) for every worker anyway, so the toolkit runs on Rocketbox bases throughout and the Mixamo clip list above is reference only.
